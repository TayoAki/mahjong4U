# Skip the Ad War, Own American Mahjong

For a small team, the most profitable mahjong opportunity is **American (NMJL-style) mahjong, sold as a subscription app for learning the game and playing it with your own group**. The much larger ad-funded mahjong-solitaire market is the wrong place to start. AppLovin's tools belong in the plan as infrastructure you rent, not as the growth engine you bet on. Solitaire holds most of the money: Vita Mahjong alone earned an estimated **$89.8M in ad revenue in January–May 2026** ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-game-ad-monetization)). But that market is a contest of user-acquisition (UA) capital. Mahjong-solitaire installs grew 189% in 2025 while category revenue fell ([AppMagic via PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-most-downloaded-mobile-games-of-2025/)). Vita was the most-advertised game on both iOS and Android for seven months running ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-februarys-top-games-vento-gets-4m-vita-mahjong-tops-ad-charts-jest-lands-7m-roblox-stats-more/)). The average casual campaign earns back only 47% of its iOS spend by day 30 ([Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/)). American mahjong turns those economics around. US Yelp searches for mahjong clubs rose **4,467%** in a year ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1018856)). Players already pay $5–7 a month or $50–60 a year. The biggest incumbent app has about 26K US ratings, and the League's own app has four. Two things can sink this plan. First, the National Mah Jongg League (NMJL) claims copyright in the "selection, coordination, compilation" of its annual card and launched a competing app in March 2026. Second, roughly 25 new American-mahjong iOS apps appeared in 2026. The plan below proposes an iOS/iPad-first app built on a rules engine that can load any card: an original house card, a licensed alternative card, and a private bring-your-own-card mode. It charges a freemium subscription plus cosmetic tile sets and uses AppLovin MAX only for light ads. An ad-funded solitaire spin-off for web portals waits until the core business passes its gates. About **$100–250K** buys a clear go/kill answer. The risks that matter most are card rights, bot quality and honesty, and keeping paid acquisition secondary to community-driven growth.

## Mahjong is four businesses, and three of them punish small teams

Neither Sensor Tower nor AppMagic tracks "mahjong" as a category. Pair-matching solitaire is filed under "Hypercasual Match Pair", and Riichi and Chinese four-player apps sit under board and card games, so any market size has to be assembled title by title ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-game-ad-monetization)). Built up that way, the four sub-genres that matter to a Western team share tiles and little else.

| Sub-genre | Core audience | Revenue model | Leader and scale | Trend | Fit for a small team |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mahjong solitaire (pair matching) | Older, female-skewing casual players; US, UK, Brazil | Ads first; remove-ads and subscriptions second | Vita Mahjong: 219.1M downloads Jan 2025–Jul 2026; $89.8M ad revenue Jan–May 2026 | Installs +189% in 2025, revenue down | Poor if funded by paid UA; workable as a niche, web or B2B product |
| Triple-match (Tile Master / Zen Match style) | Women 35+ | IAP and live events | Match Factory! holds 56% of Match 3D revenue | Tile IAP −31% in 2025; 0 of 97 new Match 3D launches above $100K/month | Avoid |
| Riichi (Japanese 4-player) | Japan-centric, younger, anime fans | Cosmetic gacha | Mahjong Soul: >50% of Japanese mobile-mahjong revenue | Mature, driven by IP collaborations | Avoid as the core product; ~3% of the audience speaks English |
| American (NMJL card) | US women ~30–75, playing in social groups | Subscriptions at $4.99–6.99/mo or $49.99–60/yr | Mahjong 4 Friends: ~25.6K US ratings | Offline boom; ~25 new iOS apps in 2026 | Best fit |

**Mahjong solitaire is huge, ad-funded and owned by one buyer.** Vita Mahjong is a classic Shanghai-style game repositioned "for seniors", with large tiles, no timers and offline play. It logged **219.1M downloads from January 2025 to July 2026** ([Singular/Sensor Tower](https://www.singular.net/blog/top-mobile-games/)) and holds **4.9★ from 1.8M US ratings** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vita-mahjong/id6468921495)). Its estimated ad revenue more than doubled year over year to $89.8M in January–May 2026, second only to Block Blast among all mobile games ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-game-ad-monetization)). Annualized, that is roughly $215M a year from the 19 tracked countries alone. Its publisher, Learnings/Oakever, was founded in 2016 by the former head of AdMob at Google China, has 200–300 staff, and has faced cloning lawsuits and Apple content disputes ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/vita-mahjong-amaze-go-and-meowdoku-maker-oakever-games-fights-cloning-accusations/)). The rising downloads and falling revenue mean volume is being bought, not earned. A newcomer can still make a living in the long tail. Kristanix's Mahjong Titan, launched in 2010, is estimated at about $10K+ a month, 71% of it from ads ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/375149734)). A solo developer's web solitaire site reached about $10K a month from ads ([IndieHustle](https://www.indiehustle.co/p/a-website-for-solitaire-making-10000)). Competing head-on with Vita through paid UA, though, is the least attractive move in the category.

**Triple-match is shrinking.** AppMagic shows IAP revenue in the "Match-3: Tile" sub-genre falling **30.87% in 2025**, from $115.1M to $79.6M ([AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)). In H1 2025, 3D triple-match revenue fell 12% on 34% fewer installs, and **none of 97 new launches passed $100K a month** ([AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-casual-games-in-h125)).

**Riichi is a Japanese gacha business with a tiny Western tail.** Mahjong Soul earned nearly $150M from November 2019 to May 2023, 90% of it in Japan ([Sensor Tower JP](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/mahjong-soul-code-geass-collaboration)). It held more than half of Japanese mobile-mahjong revenue from 2022 to 2024 and has earned roughly $20+ per download ([Dengeki Online](https://dengekionline.com/article/202406/7775)). Only about 3% of its audience speaks English, and its English Steam version averaged 419–546 concurrent players in late 2025 ([COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/2025/04/mahjong-soul-how-an-anime-mahjong-game-climbed-into-steams-top-charts/); [Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/mahjong-soul)). A Western Riichi trainer would fill a real gap, but only an enthusiast-sized one.

**American mahjong is the outlier: small, social, subscription-priced and growing.** Players use the NMJL's annual card of preset hands plus jokers, and every app must update to the new card each spring ([Bam Good Time](https://bamgoodtime.com/blog/best-american-mahjong-apps-2026)). The rest of this report argues that it is the best fit, and explains why best fit does not mean easy.

## American mahjong pairs pricing power with an unresolved card problem

### Offline demand is booming and players already pay

The NMJL has **more than 350,000 members**. One factory in Taixing went from making about 20 American sets a year to about 200 a day. Yelp searches for mahjong clubs rose 4,467% in the year to August 2025 ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1018856)). A retailer estimates the League sells 500,000–750,000 cards a year ([Atlanta Jewish Times](https://www.atlantajewishtimes.com/meet-the-real-mah-jongg-mavens/)). The Modern Mahjong Facebook group has more than 100,000 members, and its founders say players keep getting younger, down to college and high-school clubs ([J. Weekly/JTA](https://jweekly.com/2026/08/26/mah-jongg-was-a-jewish-grandmother-thing-now-teens-are-getting-in-on-the-game/)). The core audience is US women aged roughly 35–40, nicknamed "Momjong" ([The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/world/article/311510/Mahjong-mania-Game-gains-popularity-among-US-mothers-sparked-by-celebrity-influence-and-social-trends)), with Gen Z, sororities and networks of older Jewish women as further pillars ([NBC Los Angeles](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/national-international/why-mahjong-rising-popularity-los-angeles/3923210/)). The audience is heavily on iPhone and iPad: Eight Bam's trainer has 22.9K iOS ratings against just 113 Google Play reviews ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458051621)). That argues for launching on iOS first, with iPad layouts.

Reviews show both the willingness to pay and the friction. Positive examples: "Worth the $7 a month" and "You'll likely pay more than that per game at a studio" ([MahJongg4Fun](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6746219026); [Mahj Parlour](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6761298326)). Negative examples: "I'm not going to pay for something when I don't even know how to play the game yet", a trial that was "impossible to cancel", and resentment at paying again for each new card year ([Mahj Parlour](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6761298326); [Real Mah Jongg](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1249840399); [Eight Bam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458051621)). Suppose several hundred thousand people play and 2–5% pay about $50 a year. The serviceable market is then roughly **$5–12M a year**. That figure rests on an unaudited card-sales estimate and is illustrative only. Even so, it shows a small team can build a very profitable business by winning a meaningful share, with no need for Vita-scale spending.

### The incumbents are small, and several are stumbling

US App Store figures as of 2026-10-24: [Mahjong 4 Friends](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1552704332), [Eight Bam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458051621), [The Mahjong App](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6763795120), [Real Mah Jongg](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1249840399), [Mahj Parlour](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6761298326), [NMJL Online](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nmjl-online/id6476651173).

The window is open but narrowing. The Mahjong App reached **about 7.9K ratings in under six months**, which shows a well-executed newcomer can overtake apps launched between 2017 and 2021. Incumbents are stumbling. Thirty-five of Mahjong 4 Friends' latest 100 reviews are 1★ after an October 20, 2026 update broke it on iPad. NMJL Online's four reviews call it "junk" that "looks like a 90s web site" ([Mahjong 4 Friends](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1552704332); [NMJL Online](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nmjl-online/id6476651173)). But roughly 25 American-mahjong apps first launched on iOS in 2026. Bots, private friend tables, Charleston support (the pre-game tile pass), suggested hands and tutorials are now table stakes ([Bam Good Time](https://bamgoodtime.com/blog/best-american-mahjong-apps-2026)). An entrant therefore has to win on quality, exactly where reviews show pain. Players complain of bots that end "every game" as a wall game whatever the difficulty, and rules engines that wrongly call hands dead on the new card. They complain of tutorials that never explain how to call a tile, no free arranging of tiles in the rack, and small tiles on low-contrast backgrounds ([Real Mah Jongg](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1249840399); [Maylee](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758261607); [MahJongg4Fun](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6746219026); [The Mahjong App](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6763795120)). The emotional core is remote play with an existing group: "We all live in different places and play Mahjong online with one another multiple times a week" ([The Mahjong App](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6763795120)). Adjacent revenue already exists. Bam Good Time Club charges organizers $19–49 a month, Mahjy takes 2% of paid seats, and several apps sell collectible designer tile sets ([Bam Good Time](https://bamgoodtime.com/blog/best-american-mahjong-apps-2026)).

### The NMJL card is the central business risk

The League's terms of service, dated February 22, 2026, claim copyright not only in its content but in "**the selection, coordination, compilation, and enhancement of such Content ('Arrangement')**". They forbid using League content "in connection with... the development of any software program" without written consent, and they state that "the card is not available in electronic form" ([NMJL Online ToS](https://faqs.nmjlonline.com/legal/NMJL-Online_Terms-of-Service.pdf)). The League owns the NATIONAL MAH JONGG LEAGUE trademark, Reg. No. 3,249,385 ([trademark record](https://trademarkregistration.app/Trademark/Details/78936989)). Since March 11, 2026 it has sold its own app, listed on the App Store under the seller Quicksilver Software ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nmjl-online/id6476651173)).

On the other side of the ledger, I found no public licensing program, no licensed third-party app, and no reported lawsuit or takedown. One IP firm says there are "no widely reported lawsuits, but enforcement is still possible", and that the League holds registrations going back to 1982 ([TMBTQ Law](https://www.tmbtq.com/post/ip-and-mah-jongg/)). Lawyers disagree on the central question. Lerner David argues that protection covers the card's layout, typography and formatting, "not the underlying game or rules, including which hands win" ([Lerner David](https://www.lernerdavid.com/news-successes/unscripted/copyright-issues-in-america-s-fast-growing-mah-jongg-craze)). An instructor wiki asserts that reproducing hands "even in part" for distribution is a violation ([Mahj Life](https://mahjlife.com/wiki/copyright-fair-use-article-256/)). The realistic exposure is a complaint to Apple or Google, not a court verdict. Apple can pull an app while it reviews a copyright claim ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/11/20/apples-quick-app-store-takedowns-over-copyright-claims-are-a-nightmare-for-developers)). Statutory damages run $750–$30,000 per work, and up to $150,000 if the infringement is willful ([17 U.S.C. §504(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/504)). The League's own app launch and its rewrite of the terms on trade names and logos both raise its motive to act against apps that market "NMJL card" play.

Competitors have settled on four workarounds, which rank by risk:

1. **Original house card or licensed alternative card (lowest risk).** I Love Mahj, Maylee and Daily Mahj use their own cards. Others license alternative cards: Marvelous in Mahjong 4 Friends, The Big Card and Mah Jongg Press in Real Mah Jongg, and the American Mahjong Society card in The Mahjong App ([I Love Mahj](https://ilovemahj.com/kb/67000755029); [Mahjong 4 Friends](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1552704332); [Real Mah Jongg](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1249840399); [The Mahjong App](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6763795120)).
2. **Bring your own card (middle risk).** The app requires the player to own the physical card, as NMJL Online and Mahj Parlour do, or to photograph it, as Callie's does ([Bam Good Time](https://bamgoodtime.com/blog/best-american-mahjong-apps-2026); [Callie's Mahjong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6781089384)).
3. **Embed the current NMJL hands (highest risk).** Roughly 20 apps do this without disclosing a license, Bam Good Time's free app among them ([Bam Good Time Online](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6759616156)).
4. **Disclaimers.** Newer entrants add notices such as "not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Mah Jongg League and does not reproduce an official card" ([Mahjong Trainer](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6806357092)).

The technical answer is a rules engine that reads hand patterns as data. One NMJL card's 55 lines expand to 1,059 concrete hands ([Mahjong Mate](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6478509695)). Alternative cards also change the rules: Marvelous allows quints (five of a kind) of any number in a run and joker pickups for some hands ([Sloperama](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column441a.html)). Cards should therefore be data files, not code. Asking this audience to type in 50–60 pattern lines a year is too much friction, though. User-entered cards work only as a private, on-device mode next to cards the app ships itself.

## AppLovin sets the rules of the ad economy, whichever game you build

### AppLovin is now pure ad tech with a self-reinforcing loop

AppLovin sold its game studios to Tripledot to concentrate on advertising. The deal closed on June 30, 2025, for $400M in cash plus about 20% of Tripledot's equity ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250701033978/en/applovin-completes-sale-of-mobile-gaming-business-to-tripledot-studios)). What remains is:

- **MAX mediation**, which is **free to publishers because bidding networks pay AppLovin a fee** ([AppLovin](https://applovin.com/monetization));
- the **AppLovin Exchange**;
- **Axon**, its AI ad-buying platform, rebranded in October 2025, whose self-serve Ads Manager opened to all advertisers in mid-2026 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/applovin-rebrands-consumer-facing-ads-offering-to-axon));
- **Adjust**, its mobile measurement partner (MMP).

The company is enormous and very profitable. FY2025 revenue was $5.48B, with $4.51B of adjusted EBITDA ([AppLovin 4Q25 release](https://s21.q4cdn.com/165405286/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/v2/4Q25-AppLovin-Press-Release.pdf)). Q2 2026 revenue was $1.92B at about an 84% adjusted EBITDA margin ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/applovin-revenue-growth-slows-to-53-as-q3-margin-guidance-drops-to-83/)). It took **36% of global mobile-game ad revenue** from January 2025 to May 2026, against AdMob's 29% ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-game-ad-monetization)).

The strategic point for a developer is the loop. A consultant reports that AppLovin restricts its ROAS campaigns (bidding to a target return on ad spend) to MAX users ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/nine-facts-about-the-mediation-space-in-2025/)). A 2026 guide is more precise: Ad ROAS and Blended ROAS campaigns require MAX for all mediation, while IAP-only ROAS campaigns can run with other mediators ([SuperScale](https://superscale.com/resources/applovin-axon-for-advertisers)). For an ad-funded game, then, the impression-level ad revenue that MAX sees directly trains the bidder that buys installs. That loop compounds for whoever spends the most. It explains Vita's scale, and it is why a small team should treat MAX as plumbing before betting its UA budget on Axon. There is also counterparty risk: short-seller reports in early 2025 were followed by a reported SEC probe into AppLovin's data collection, with no wrongdoing alleged ([Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/AppLovin+probed+by+US+SEC+over+data-collection+practices%2C+Bloomberg+News+reports/25424385.html)). One structural caution applies to any ad-first plan. Game ad revenue has grown about 16% a year since 2020, against about 3% for IAP, yet the largest IAP-first hybrid games still earn about 4x the largest ad-first ones ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/5-numbers-hiding-in-plain-sight-sensor-towers-ad-monetization-report)).

### Default to MAX and keep Google as the hedge

GameBiz Consulting sampled top AppMagic titles in May 2025. MAX ran in **73.1% of top-downloaded and 55% of top-grossing ad-monetized games**, and MAX, LevelPlay and AdMob together held over 90% of the market ([PocketGamer.biz/GameBiz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/nine-facts-about-the-mediation-space-in-2025/)). The same consultancy found that AppLovin and Unity each perform about 4x better on their own mediation, and that AppLovin does not bid on LevelPlay. It also noted that networks with SDK bidders must run bidding-only in MAX from July 2025 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/nine-facts-about-the-mediation-space-in-2025/)). LevelPlay's case weakened further in August 2026. Reporting on Unity's sale of Supersonic said Unity is exiting non-core, lower-margin businesses, including winding down the ironSource Ads network ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tripledot-buys-supersonic-for-about-40m-from-unity-exclusive/)).

AdMob is the credible alternative and the essential hedge. AppLovin, Meta Audience Network, Mintegral, Moloco, Pangle, Unity and others can all bid through it ([AdMob](https://admob.google.co.in/home/bidding/bidding-partners/)). Vendor case studies on both platforms credit added auction density with raising ARPDAU (average revenue per daily active user): +20% for Tripledot's Solitaire on MAX and +10–15% for Me2Zen on AdMob ([AppLovin](https://blog.applovin.com/?p=24819); [AdMob](https://admob.google.co.in/home/resources/metwozen-sees-fifteen-percent-arpdau-increase-admob-platform-bidding/)). Estimates of integration effort differ. AppLovin says most publishers integrate "in under a day". A 2026 guide estimates 2–4 weeks plus 60–90 days of testing and calls 100K+ daily active users (DAU) the best fit ([AppLovin](https://applovin.com/monetization); [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/applovin-publisher-monetization-guide)). Below about 50K DAU, mediation A/B tests have little statistical power. A small team should launch bidding-only with 6–8 bidders and leave fine-tuning for later.

### Geography moves eCPMs more than placement does

eCPM is ad revenue per 1,000 impressions.

| Format | US / North America eCPM | Global blended (Q4 2024) | Low-tier example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rewarded video | ~$13.6 iOS / ~$9.0 Android (NA, Appodeal Q4 2024) up to $30.25 iOS / $27.88 Android (US, Tenjin Q1 2024) | ~$3.0–4.3 | LATAM ~$3.3 iOS / ~$1.8 Android |
| Interstitial | ~$10.4 iOS / ~$9.6 Android (NA) up to $19.62 (US, Tenjin) | ~$1.6–2.4 | LATAM ~$2.2 iOS / ~$1.3 Android |
| Banner | ~$0.4–1.2 | ~$0.1 | — |

Sources: [Appodeal Q4 2024 report](https://appodeal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Quarterly_Mobile_eCPM_Report_-_Q4_2024.pdf) (chart readings); [Tenjin via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/tenjin-ad-monetization-in-mobile); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026).

The benchmarks disagree by more than 2x, but the pattern is consistent: country and platform dominate. **US iOS rewarded eCPMs fell from about $18 in December to $11–12.50 by mid-January**, and peak-to-trough swings run 30–40%. The US produced 58% of iOS and 32% of Android game ad revenue in Q2 2026. Gaming had the highest App Tracking Transparency (ATT) opt-in rate, 39% in Q1 2026 ([Playio, citing Tenjin and Adjust](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)).

Ad ARPDAU equals impressions per DAU × eCPM ÷ 1,000. As an illustration, a US-heavy game showing 3 interstitials at $12, 1.5 rewarded videos at $15 and 50 banner impressions at $0.50 per DAU earns **about $0.08 per DAU**. The same mix at global blended rates earns roughly $0.01–0.02. Casual games earn the highest D90 ad ARPU of any genre, $0.55 per user ([AppsFlyer via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appsflyer-app-monetization-in-2026)). Older US players are valuable mainly because they cluster in the highest-eCPM country and platform and play for long stretches. An ironSource survey of 1,533 US adults aged 50+ found that 42% play more than an hour a day and 52% are likely to engage with in-game rewards ([Unity/ironSource](https://unity.com/blog/beyond-50-an-untapped-audience)). No source measures eCPM by age directly.

### Design ads around older players' patience, not maximum fill

Puzzle games tolerate ads well. They take 53% of mobile-game ad revenue ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-game-ad-monetization)), and Appodeal's experts say puzzle and match games "can handle frequent interstitial and banner ads" ([App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/Mobile-Casual-Benchmarks-Report-2025/)). The only public A/B result found is old: adding interstitials from day one to a rewarded-plus-banner setup raised ARPDAU 18% and cut D7 retention 2.5% ([Unity/ironSource, 2019](https://unity.com/resources/zimad-dig-out)).

Vita's sequence is the reference design. It shows no ads or offers in the first hour. From level 11 it shows banners and an interstitial after each level, levels run about 5 minutes, and a $5.99 "No Ads" purchase sits in the options menu ([Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/1-hour-analysis-vita-mahjong-vita-studio/)). Rewarded video unlocks Shuffle and Hint ([Balancy](https://balancy.co/blog/2025/06/17/monetization-and-gameplay-analysis-of-top-downloaded-vita-mahjong/)). Meta suggests 90–120 seconds between interstitials for puzzle games ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/games/monetize/in-app-ads/interstitial-ads)), and Appodeal advises no more than one a minute ([Appodeal](https://docs.appodeal.com/publishing/ad-types/ad-placements-best-practices)). Google Play bans interstitials that appear unexpectedly, at the start of a level, before the splash screen, or that cannot be closed after 15 seconds ([Google Play Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753)).

Price the remove-ads purchase carefully. A heavily engaged US player worth $0.08 a day for 180 days generates about $14 of ad revenue, so a $5.99 "remove all ads" purchase can lose money. Remove interstitials and banners, and keep opt-in rewarded ads, which players still choose to watch.

The pre-launch plumbing is not optional:

- **Consent:** a Google-certified consent management platform (CMP) for EEA/UK traffic (required since January 16, 2024) and for Switzerland, listing every mediation partner. Without it, Google serves only "Limited Ads" ([AdMob](https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/gdpr)).
- **app-ads.txt** at the root of the developer website named in the store listing ([AdMob Help](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9363762)).
- **SKAdNetwork IDs** for every network in the iOS Info.plist, plus early SDK initialization ([AppLovin](https://support.applovin.com/en/godot/overview/integration)).
- **Tax forms and payouts:** AppLovin pays monthly, about 15 days after month-end (NET 15), once earnings pass $100. Every other mediated network pays separately ([AppLovin payment FAQ](https://support.applovin.com/en/max/faq/faq-applovin-monetization-payment)).

## The benchmarks that decide whether a cohort pays back

### Target top-decile retention, not the median

GameAnalytics' 2025 data across 16,000+ games puts median D1 retention at about 22%, D7 just under 4% and D30 at 0.68–0.79%. The top decile reaches about 40% on D1 and 11–12% on D7, and median daily playtime is about 12 minutes against 40–42 minutes for the top decile ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks)). Sensor Tower estimates for leading puzzle titles are far higher: Candy Crush 39.9% D1, 22.6% D7 and 12.5% D30; Block Blast 53.5%, 33.4% and 21.7% ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-gaming-market-in)). Vita is estimated at 46% D1 and 26% D7 ([FoxData](https://foxdata.com/jp/blogs/how-vita-mahjong-stayed-1-in-the-us-app-store-and-what-it-means-for-app-growth-in-2025/)).

On monetization, casual games earn D90 IAP ARPU of $1.34, ARPPU (revenue per paying user) of $7.26 and ad ARPU of $0.55, roughly a 70/30 IAP/ads split. Paid UA drives 61% of casual IAP revenue ([AppsFlyer via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appsflyer-app-monetization-in-2026)). Ads are 22.1% of game revenue in the US but 70.3% in India ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/07/06/in-2025-mobile-game-advertising-revenue-exceeded-12-billion-analytics)).

Subscriptions are gaming's weakest model on average. RevenueCat's gaming medians are:

- download-to-paid conversion of 1.0% (top quartile above 2.3%);
- download-to-trial of 4.4%;
- trial-to-paid of 25% (top quartile above 39.8%);
- realized first-year revenue per payer of $11.22;
- prices of $4.99 a month and $24.99 a year, with weekly plans making up 82% of gaming subscriptions sold ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-gaming)).

Across all categories, higher-priced apps convert better by D35 (2.7% against 1.5%), and cheap annual plans retain up to 36% of subscribers after a year, against 6.7% for high-priced monthly plans ([Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)). American-mahjong apps charge about twice the gaming median and serve a niche, high-intent audience. No source publishes their conversion rates, and that is the number Phase 2 of the plan has to measure.

### Average paid UA loses money in casual

Liftoff's Singular-based data puts average casual CPI at $1.41 on iOS and $0.14 on Android. Those are blended global figures that include cheap markets. Average D30 ROAS is 47% on iOS and 15% on Android ([Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/)). Older Liftoff data put puzzle D7 ROAS at 6.9% ([Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/casual-games)). The planning formula is: target D7 ROAS = target ROAS ÷ (revenue at payback day ÷ revenue at D7). For example, 120% by D180 with a 6x multiple requires 20% D7 ROAS ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/d7-roas-benchmarks-mobile-games)).

The arithmetic is unforgiving for a generic solitaire game. Take a US iOS CPI of $2–4 against a casual D90 ARPU of $1.89. Even with an assumed 1.6–2.0x multiple from D90 to D365, lifetime value lands at about $3.0–3.8, so an average game does not pay back. At a blended ARPDAU of $0.15, a $3 install needs roughly 25 active days after store fees. An ad-only game at $0.08 needs far more. Only Vita-grade retention clears that bar.

The American subscription case looks different but carries its own warning. A $49.99 annual plan nets $42.49 after Apple's 15% small-business commission. If 3% of installs pay about $40 in their first year, revenue per install is about $1.20. Covering a $3 CPI would need about 7.5% install-to-paid conversion, or several years of renewals. **That is why paid UA in American mahjong should supplement community growth, bought only on channels whose cohorts prove they pay back.**

### Price anchors, store fees and the web-checkout window

Observed price anchors:

- **Royal Match:** smallest coin pack $1.99; monthly pass $9.99 ([GameMakers](https://www.gamemakers.com/p/royal-matchs-219-battle-pass-secret)).
- **Vita:** remove-ads $5.99; subscriptions at $2.99 weekly, $5.99 monthly and $19.99 yearly ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vita-mahjong/id6468921495)).
- **Microsoft Solitaire Premium:** $1.99 a month or $9.99 a year ([App Store](https://apple.co/2gCnkVc)).
- **American-mahjong apps:** $3.99–6.99 a month, $17.99–59.99 a year, and a $149.99 lifetime tier.

Both stores charge 15% on a small developer's first $1M: Apple through its Small Business Program ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/?page=2)) and Google on the first $1M of annual earnings ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10632485)).

Linking out to web purchases is in flux:

- **Apple (US).** Apple has charged 0% on US link-out purchases since the 2025 contempt ruling. On December 11, 2025 the Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt finding but allowed a fee limited to Apple's genuine costs ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/25-2935/25-2935-2025-12-11.html)). Apple has proposed 5–15% tiers, with 5% for small-business developers ([Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/apple/news-apple-external-purchase-fee-epic-court-fight/)). The Supreme Court has agreed to review the case ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324312/20260813/apple-must-now-justify-app-store-commission-to-judge-who-called-it-contemptuous.htm)).
- **Google (US).** Google has allowed US external links and alternative billing since October 29, 2025, and requires enrolled developers to report and pay fees from October 1, 2026 ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)). Reports of the fee level conflict. Coda reports 20% on IAP for new installs and 10% on subscriptions. Appcharge says the settlement was withdrawn and link-outs remain at 0% ([Coda](https://www.coda.co/blog/epic-v-google-policy-update-2026/); [Appcharge](https://appcharge.com/blog/epic-v-google-the-settlement-is-off-the-table)).

Direct-to-consumer web shops are already about a **$17B channel, roughly 15% of mobile-game IAP, with a median 15% revenue uplift** ([Appcharge via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260624293221/en/New-Appcharge-Research-Direct-to-Consumer-is-Already-a-$17Bn-Market-in-Mobile-Gaming-and-the-Rest-of-the-App-Economy-is-Set-to-Follow)). Providers such as Stash charge 5% plus payment costs ([Stash](https://www.stash.gg/online-gaming-payments)). An app selling $50 annual plans to loyal, community-driven players should add US web checkout once revenue is meaningful, and should model both a 0% and a 15–20% link-out fee.

## Distribution has to be cheap before it can be scaled

### US installs cost $2–5, and nobody publishes a mahjong-specific CPI

Admiral Media's 2025 campaign data puts casual-puzzle CPI at $1.50–3.50 on iOS and $0.80–2.00 on Android ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/mobile-game-marketing-benchmarks/)). Adjust reports that North American gaming CPI rose 31% to $1.68 in 2025, with North American CPMs at $15.98, the highest in the world ([FoxData citing Adjust](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/2026-mobile-game-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-how-much-should-you-spend/)). On Apple Ads, the median US Games CPI is **$4.63** ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)) and the average Games cost per tap is $4.02 ([MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/report/apple-ads-2026-benchmark-report/executive-summary/)). Benchmarks disagree by up to 10x, because some blend in India and Southeast Asia. Plan on roughly $2–4 on iOS and $1–3 on Android for US traffic. No source publishes CPIs for mahjong players or for women over 50.

### Meta finds the audience; AppLovin scales ad-funded games

AppsFlyer's 2025 Performance Index ranks Apple Ads first for iOS gaming globally and Google Ads first for Android gaming, with AppLovin leading iOS gaming in Tier-1 North America and Western Europe ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/performance-index-2025/)). AppLovin's reach in puzzle is surging: its share of Royal Match's ad impressions rose from 21.5% to 50.6% in a year. Facebook remains Vita Mahjong's largest network, at 27.6% of impressions ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-game-advertising-in)). Axon requires an MMP before any campaign runs. It publishes no minimum spend but needs enough budget for 15–20 target events a day. Its goals run on D7 or D28 windows and should be changed in roughly 5% steps. Its lead creatives are 9:16 videos of up to 60 seconds plus a playable ad ([SuperScale](https://superscale.com/resources/applovin-axon-for-advertisers)). Google App Campaigns need a daily budget of at least 50x the target CPI ([Admiral Media](https://admiral.media/google-app-campaigns-best-practices/)).

For American mahjong, the sensible order is:

1. community channels;
2. Apple Ads on exact-match "american mahjong" searches;
3. Meta campaigns aimed at US women interested in the game;
4. AppLovin, only for an ad-funded spin-off that already runs MAX and has ad-revenue history.

### Creative volume wins, but health claims and fake gameplay get banned

AppsFlyer's analysis of 1.1M video creatives found that **the top 2% of gaming creatives took 53% of spend** in Q1 2025. Ads showing "pure success" outcomes got about 33% higher IPM (installs per 1,000 impressions) on ad networks, while "pure failure" outcomes got about 65% higher IPM on social channels. User-generated gameplay reviews reached 22% D7 retention on ad networks ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/creative-optimization-report-2025/)). The median top US game advertiser launched **465 new creatives a month in Q1 2026**, against 135 a year earlier ([Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-game-advertising-in)). Freelance creatives cost $150–350 per variation ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)).

Vita scaled with AI-generated doctors and an Einstein-style figure making brain claims ([Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/vita-mahjong-scaled-past-150k-a-day/)). On December 4, 2024, the UK Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) upheld a complaint against a Vita ad featuring a man in medical scrubs. The ad claimed the game was "designed Exclusively for people with memory loss and sleep loss" ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/vita-studio-pte-ltd-g24-1253610-vita-studio-pte-ltd.html)). The ASA also treats footage that misrepresents the core game as misleading, even with a disclaimer ([ASA, Evony ruling](https://asa.org.uk/rulings/top-games-inc-a23-1213924-top-games-inc.html)). A small team can still compete on concept count. Ten to twenty cheap variants a week, cut from real gameplay and framed around comfort, readability and social play rather than medical benefit, is achievable and policy-safe.

### Test in stages and measure with free-tier tools

Game Growth Advisor's soft-launch guide recommends running two markets in parallel: a Tier-1 English proxy such as the UK, Canada or Australia, and a cheap volume market in LATAM or Southeast Asia. Its other benchmarks:

- **Cost:** a lean run of 4 weeks costs **$11–23K**; a full 12-week program costs $62–110K.
- **Sample size:** about 900 installs per test cell are needed to read a 30% D1 within ±3 points.
- **Kill rules:** stop buying if Android D1 is below 25% after three builds; stop scaling if D7 is below 8% or D30 is below 3%.
- **Scale test:** triple spend; if CPI rises less than 30% the channel can scale, and if it rises more than 50% it is saturated.
- **Google Play gate:** new personal developer accounts need 12 testers opted in for 14 days, which adds 3–5 weeks.

Source for all of the above: [Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/). An American-mahjong app has only one real market, the US, so it should soft-launch there directly on small budgets.

On measurement, gaming ATT opt-in among users shown the prompt was 37.9% in Q1 2025, and about 32% in the US ([Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/att-opt-in-rates-2025)). SKAdNetwork 4 reached majority adoption only in 2026 ([Kochava](https://www.kochava.com/blog/your-ios-attribution-strategy-2026-reality-check/)), and production use of AdAttributionKit is still early ([Singular](https://www.singular.net/blog/scaling-ios-apps-in-2026-webinar-qa/)). Google retired most Privacy Sandbox technologies on October 17, 2025 ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/10/20/google-is-retiring-privacy-sandbox/)). Measurement is cheap at small scale:

- **Tenjin:** 2,000 free paid conversions a month, then $0.04 each ([Tenjin](https://tenjin.com/pricing)).
- **AppsFlyer:** 12,000 free conversions in the first year, then $0.07 ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/pricing/)).
- **Singular:** 15,000 free conversions, then $0.05 ([Airbridge comparison](https://www.airbridge.io/tips/mobile-attribution-pricing-plans-contract-terms)).

No MMP is needed until paid UA begins.

### ASO and community do the heavy lifting in a niche

On iOS, the title and subtitle get 30 characters each and the keyword field 100. The long description is not indexed ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/how-to-optimize-your-ios-keyword-field)). Since July 2025, custom product pages can appear in organic search for keywords assigned to them, and the limit has been raised to 70 per app ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/custom-product-pages-app-store/)). Google Play allows up to 50 custom store listings. Google reports a median 10% conversion lift when they are used with Google Ads ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-playtime-recap-google-plays-vision-for-2025)). Solitaire listings stack "mahjong" with "tile", "match" and "classic", plus hooks such as "large tiles", "for seniors" and "no ads". "American mahjong" is a separate keyword intent with a separate audience.

Distribution in that niche runs through communities. Mahjong content sites attract large audiences: themahjong.com counted about 1.68M unique users in a year ([themahjong.com](https://themahjong.com/blog/who-solves-puzzles-a-study-of-the-online-puzzle-game-audience)). Bam Good Time's directory lists more than 1,400 clubs ([Bam Good Time](https://bamgoodtime.com/blog/best-american-mahjong-apps-2026)), and every spring's new card creates a natural moment to acquire and re-engage players. Teachers and club organizers are the cheapest acquisition channel available. I found no metrics on that channel, so the plan treats it as a hypothesis to test early.

**Unity is the lowest-risk engine.** The runtime fee was cancelled before it was ever charged. Unity Personal is free until trailing revenue plus funding passes $200K ([Unity](https://unity.com/products/pricing-updates)). For an ad-heavy game, that threshold can arrive at only about 10K DAU, so budget for Pro seats early. The alternatives work but carry costs:

- **Godot 4** now has an official AppLovin MAX plugin, though iOS setup still needs manual native steps. It has no LevelPlay plugin, relies on community plugins for AdMob and Firebase, and gained Foundation-maintained Play Billing and StoreKit 2 plugins in April 2026 ([AppLovin-MAX-Godot](https://github.com/AppLovin/AppLovin-MAX-Godot); [Godot](https://godotengine.org/article/godot-mobile-update-apr-2026/)).
- **Defold** ships official MAX, LevelPlay, AdMob, Firebase and IAP extensions, although its MAX extension does not run on HTML5 ([Defold](https://defold.com/extension-applovin); [Defold newsletter](https://gamedev.net/news/5363-defold-summer-newsletter-2026/)).
- **Flutter** has official MAX and AdMob plugins ([AppLovin-MAX-Flutter](https://github.com/AppLovin/AppLovin-MAX-Flutter); [Google AdMob](https://developers.google.com/admob)).

Whatever the engine, Google Play now requires new apps and updates to target Android 16 (API 36), and the iOS minimum has risen to 15.0 ([Godot 3.6.3 notes](https://godotengine.org/article/maintenance-release-godot-3-6-3/)).

**The American mode needs a server you trust.** Four-player mahjong hides each player's hand and the wall. If clients hold that data, a modified client can read opponents' tiles. The server must therefore shuffle, deal and validate every move, which rules out pure relay services. Nakama's authoritative match handlers run Go, TypeScript or Lua and document turn-based patterns ([Nakama](https://heroiclabs.com/docs/nakama/concepts/multiplayer/authoritative/)). Colyseus Cloud starts at $15 a month with no CCU limits ([Colyseus](https://colyseus.io/pricing/)). Photon costs $125 a month for 500 CCU ([Photon](https://www.photonengine.com/fusion/pricing)). PlayFab is out: its free tier was cut to 1,000 lifetime users in March 2026 ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-za/xbox/playfab/pricing/development-mode)).

No American-mahjong scoring library turned up in the research, so expect to write your own. The MIT-licensed `mahjong` package covers only Riichi ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mahjong/)). Mortal, a strong Riichi AI, is AGPL-3.0, which makes it risky to embed in a closed commercial server ([GitHub](https://github.com/Equim-chan/Mortal)). My engineering recommendation: define each card as a JSON file in a small pattern language. Interpret it with one matcher on the server (TypeScript) and one on the client (C#, for offline bots and coaching), and run both against a shared set of known-answer test hands. That way a new card can ship on release day without code changes.

**The solitaire spin-off is technically modest.** Build each board in reverse: choose a removal order on the empty layout, then lay tile pairs onto it, so every deal is solvable by construction ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/dev_nash/most-free-mahjong-games-can-deal-you-a-board-you-cant-finish-i-built-mine-backwards-1cia)). Grade difficulty by how often a solver succeeds on the board; deciding solvability in general is NP-complete ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.6559)). For art, the FluffyStuff tile set is CC0 but lacks the flower and season tiles ([GitHub](https://github.com/FluffyStuff/riichi-mahjong-tiles)). The xmahjongg tile sets are GPL or "non-profit only" ([MIT](https://stuff.mit.edu/afs/sipb/user/dmaze/src/mj-1.6-src/tiles-v1/README)).

At 10K–100K DAU, tooling rises to roughly $300–2,500 a month, plus Unity Pro seats. The main cost lines are MMP fees and RevenueCat's 1%, which is about $450 a month at $45K of monthly revenue.

## Most legal exposure comes from monetization choices you control

**Keep coins away from outcomes.** Apple bars using in-app purchases to buy currency for real-money gaming of any kind. Real-money gaming apps must be licensed, geo-restricted and free to download ([Apple Guidelines 5.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google bars apps from accepting wagers or in-app currency to win prizes of real-world value ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)). In *Kater v. Churchill Downs* (9th Cir. 2018), Big Fish Casino's chips, which could not be cashed out, were still held to be a "thing of value" under Washington gambling law ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/plaintiff-rolls-a-win-in-ninth-circuit-gambling-op)). The riskiest design in mahjong is therefore four-player tables where purchased coins are staked on hands. Sweepstakes models are now banned outright in several states. California's AB 831 took effect on January 1, 2026, and New York's S5935-A carries fines of $10,000–100,000 per violation ([SBC Americas, California](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/10/14/newsom-signs-california-sweepstakes-ban/); [SBC Americas, New York](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/12/08/new-york-hochul-signs-sweepstakes-ban/)).

**Bots must be honest.** In *Skillz v. Papaya*, a jury found that Papaya had sold bot-filled cash games as fair human competition. The court then entered a **$719M disgorgement judgment** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://pocketgamer.biz/court-orders-papaya-gaming-to-pay-skillz-719m-in-false-advertising-case); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/skillz-v-papaya-gaming-record-lanham-act-verdict-puts-bots-matchmaking-and-skill)). Any app that fills empty seats with bots should label them, and should never claim "real players" when they are not.

**Ratings and consumer law.** Under PEGI's 2026 criteria, paid random items default to PEGI 16 and games that closely resemble gambling get PEGI 18. Login streaks earn PEGI 7, and mechanics that take away earned content for not returning earn PEGI 12 ([AFP via Ada Derana](https://adaderana.lk/technology/119714/games-with-loot-boxes-to-get-minimum-16-age-rating-across-europe)). Both stores require odds to be disclosed before any random purchase, and Apple forbids purchased currency from expiring ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)). Brazil has banned loot boxes in games likely to be used by minors since March 2026, with fines of up to 10% of Brazilian revenue ([Demarest](https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-nova-lei-de-protecao-de-criancas-e-adolescentes-no-ambiente-digital/)). The FTC's Genshin settlement cost $20M ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/genshin-impact-maker-fined-20m-over-deceptive-loot-boxes-and-child-privacy-violation)). EU consumer authorities now treat in-game currency as real money for pricing-transparency purposes ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/european-consumer-protection-network-issues-new-key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies-impact-for-gaming-and-gambling-entities-in-belgium-the-eu-and-beyond/)). The simplest compliant design sells only direct items, bundles, ad removal and subscriptions, priced in real currency.

**Age signals are becoming actual knowledge.** The baseline privacy obligations are:

- ATT may not be gated or incentivized ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)).
- Apple has rejected builds that lack privacy manifests since May 1, 2024 ([Bitrise](https://bitrise.io/blog/post/enforcement-of-apple-privacy-manifest-starting-from-may-1-2024)).
- Google's Data safety form must cover every third-party SDK ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)).
- The amended COPPA rule required compliance by April 22, 2026 ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)).

The new layer is age assurance. Texas SB 2420 has been enforceable since the Fifth Circuit stayed the injunction against it on May 28, 2026 ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/)). It requires developers to use store-supplied age data and to notify the store of significant changes ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law)). California's AB 1043 takes effect on January 1, 2027. Under it, receiving the operating system's age signal counts as actual knowledge of a user's age, with penalties of up to $2,500 per child for negligent violations and $7,500 for intentional ones ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/faq-digital-age-assurance-act-california-youth-safety-on-the-internet)). Google plans to roll out its Play Age Signals API worldwide by the end of 2026 ([Betanews](https://betanews.com/article/google-play-age-signals-api-global-rollout/)). Build an age-band router from day one: full ads and IAP for adults, non-personalized ads for teens, and contextual or no ads for under-13s.

**IP and naming.** "Mahjong" is generic: USPTO registrations routinely disclaim the word ([trademark records](https://trademarkregistration.app/Trademark/Details/75322329)). Activision's SHANGHAI registration was cancelled in 2020 ([trademark record](https://trademarkregistration.app/Trademark/Details/74468336)), though common-law rights should still be checked. *Tetris v. Xio* shows that game rules are free to use but copying a game's expressive choices infringes ([Willamette](https://wlo.willamette.edu/ip/2012/05/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc.html)). Apple's guidelines 4.1 and 4.3 reject copycats and reskins in saturated categories ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Never put "NMJL", "National Mah Jongg League" or "official" in the app's name, icon or keywords.

**Business hygiene.** Apple has removed EU listings without Digital Services Act trader status since February 17, 2025, and it publishes the trader's address and phone number ([SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/?p=55860); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-compliance-information/)). Form a company first, so a business address appears instead of a home address. Apps with accounts must offer in-app account deletion plus a web deletion link ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)).

## Self-publish the core app; borrow distribution selectively

**The publisher market is consolidating.** Tripledot paid $800M for AppLovin's 10 studios, including Lion Studios, and claims nearly $2B in annual gross revenue and 25M+ DAU ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/tripledot-completes-landmark-800m-deal-for-10-applovin-studios/)). It bought Supersonic from Unity for about $40M in August 2026. Its CEO said winning now takes "UA expertise, capital, and data science/AI capabilities, which small and mid-sized studios increasingly struggle to build alone" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tripledot-buys-supersonic-for-about-40m-from-unity-exclusive/)).

Voyer Law's dataset of 100+ publishing agreements, mostly premium games, shows developers keeping an average 58.2% of revenue with an advance and 67.9% without one. The median advance is $300K, developers keep their IP in 96–100% of deals, and terms average 6.84 years ([Voyer Law via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/voyer-law-publishing-agreements-in)). Mobile hybrid-casual publishers instead pay for UA and split profit. One vendor claims splits of 70/30 to 90/10 in the publisher's favor ([CAS.AI](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)). Their prototype gates come from the hypercasual era: Supersonic's 2021 bar was CPI under $0.30 and Android D1 of at least 38% ([Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/the-3-most-important-kpis-for-testing-your-hyper-casual-prototype)). That CPI is unrealistic for Tier-1 mahjong players. Hit rates are brutal. Only 1.4% of one casual genre's H1 2025 launches passed $100K a month ([AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-h1-2025/?hl=en)). A vendor claims 1 in 100 indie games reaches $10K a month ([CAS.AI](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)). American mahjong grows through clubs, teachers and search rather than bought volume, which is exactly where a hybrid-casual publisher adds least. **Self-publish it.**

**Web and B2B channels fit the solitaire spin-off.** Mahjong is Arkadium's most popular category, with more than 375M games played in a year ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/arkadium-celebrates-23-years-with-record-breaking-game-releases-and-key-milestones-fueling-company-growth)). Arkadium powers 300+ game channels, including AARP, The Washington Post, USA Today and the Daily Mail, and opened that network to outside developers in 2024 ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/arkadium-opens-its-vast-distribution-network-to-developers-worldwide)). It added third-party publishing backed by a $1M fund ([Business Wire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20240809092272/en/Arkadium-Unveils-New-Third-Party-Publishing-for-Game-Developers-Alongside-its-%e2%80%98APF1%e2%80%99-%e2%80%93-One-Million-Dollar-Fund/)). Poki splits revenue from its own traffic 50/50, pays 100% on traffic the developer brings, and requires web exclusivity ([Poki](https://sdk.poki.com/)). CrazyGames' 2026 jam terms give developers 60% of ad revenue and 70% of IAP. A decent casual web game typically earns $200–3,000 a month ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-monetization); [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-poki)). These channels reach an older audience without paid UA and produce retention data for free. Apple Arcade has cut upfront fees and favors licensed IP ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/inside-apple-arcade-axed-games-declining-payouts-disillusioned-studios-and-an-uncertain-future/)). The median indie Steam game earned about $249 in 2025 ([ziva.sh](https://ziva.sh/blogs/indie-game-revenue)).

**What it costs and what it is worth.** Dev shops quote £50–150K for a polished casual puzzle game, built by 3–5 developers over about 6 months ([Oceanview Games](https://oceanviewgames.co.uk/blog/posts/mobile-game-development-cost-2026); [Cubix](https://www.cubix.co/blog/mobile-game-development-cost/)). UA agencies reportedly turn down accounts spending under $10–30K a month ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)).

Exit comparables:

- **Easybrain** sold to Miniclip for $1.2B, about **4.1x revenue and 10.1x EBITDA** ([InvestGame](https://investgame.net/news/digest/2024-46/)).
- **Zen Match** sold to Moon Active for a reported $100–150M with over $100M in annual revenue, roughly 1–1.5x revenue ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/coin-master-maker-moon-active-has-acquired-zen-match-for-a-reported-100-150m/)).
- **Brainium**, whose games include solitaire and mahjong, fetched $70M upfront at about 2M DAU ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-10-13/mgm-backed-playstudios-pays-70-million-for-solitaire-maker)).
- **Small apps** reportedly sell for 12–18x monthly profit ([CAS.AI](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)).

Casual studios made up 65% of games deals through H1 2025 ([InvestGame via Game Industry Library](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/the-great-mobile-reversal-why-buyers-pay-billions-for-what-vcs-abandoned?page=2)). Buyers exist, but a small app sells for roughly one year's profit. **Build for cash flow first; an exit is a bonus.**

## The recommended plan: a subscription American-mahjong app first, an ad-funded spin-off second

### What to build

Build an **iOS and iPad-first American mahjong app for learning the game and playing it with your own group**, with Android and web following. Each core feature answers a pain point documented in competitor reviews:

1. **Bots that feel human.** Skill and wall-game frequency are tunable, and bots are always labeled as bots.
2. **A rules engine that explains itself.** It tells players why they cannot call a tile and why a hand is dead, and it is tested against each new card on release day.
3. **Comfortable tile handling.** Tiles can be arranged freely in the rack, the app works in both orientations, and large tiles, high contrast and tile labels are on by default.
4. **Private friend tables,** both real-time and turn-based.
5. **Interactive lessons,** including a Charleston tutor, free from the start.

On the card, ship an **original house card free** as the learning path. **License at least one alternative card** (Marvelous, Mahjong Press, The Big Card, AMJA or AMS) before launch. Offer a **private, on-device "my card" mode** whose patterns are never shared between users. Ask the NMJL for a license, but embed its hands only with that license or with counsel's written sign-off and a takedown contingency. Never use NMJL Online or its data to build anything, because its terms forbid it.

### How it makes money

The core is a freemium subscription. Free users get lessons, a daily game, a few bot games a day, and the right to join a friend's table. **Unlimited** costs **$6.99 a month or $49.99 a year**, with a 7-day trial on the annual plan, and covers hosting tables, unlimited bot play, coaching and every licensed card. A $149.99 lifetime tier is worth testing. Hosting is the paid feature and joining is free. Because one subscriber can bring three non-paying friends to every table, each subscriber becomes an acquisition channel. That loop is my inference: the research found no data on it.

On top of the subscription:

- **Cosmetic designer tile sets,** sold directly and never as random packs.
- **Light ads for free users through MAX:** a banner or MREC on menus, and an opt-in rewarded video for an extra daily game. No interstitials during play.
- **Phase 3 additions:** a club and teacher tier priced against the $19–49 a month that organizers already pay, a US web checkout for annual plans, and an optional take-rate on paid events.

### The stack

The client runs on **Unity 6.3 LTS**. Tables run on a **TypeScript Colyseus server** (Cloud from $15 a month) or self-hosted Nakama. Cards are JSON data in a pattern language, interpreted by matched TypeScript and C# matchers that share one test suite. The rest of the stack is RevenueCat, AppLovin MAX with Google bidding, Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics and Remote Config, GameAnalytics free, and Tenjin once paid UA starts. The tile art is commissioned so the product looks distinctive and differs visibly from competitors. A team of web developers could swap the Unity client for a TypeScript client and share one rules codebase. That trades away first-party ad SDK support, which this plan treats as a minor revenue line anyway.

## Conclusion

The size of the mahjong ad market is exactly what makes it hostile to newcomers. AppLovin's design loop lets impression revenue seen by MAX train the bidder that buys installs, so the advantage compounds for the biggest spender. Vita's roughly $215M-a-year ad run rate is therefore a moat, not evidence of open opportunity. For a small team, AppLovin is best used as infrastructure to rent: mediation, payouts and eventually ROAS campaigns, once its own D28 cohorts prove ad revenue covers CPI. It is not the strategy itself. The profit pool a small team can actually reach sits where the product is social and the customer pays directly. In that pool the competitive weapon is software quality (bots, rules accuracy, accessibility), not media budget.

The NMJL's March 2026 app is both a threat and a validation. Its 1.75★ rating shows that official status does not guarantee quality, while its terms of service show the League is now paying attention. The card strategy decides whether a quality edge can be turned into revenue, so it has to be settled with counsel and alternative-card publishers before any code is written. The spring 2027 card season is the natural first test, and spring 2028 is the first season to scale into.
