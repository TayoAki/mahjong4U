Mahjong App PRD
Oct 24, 2026 · @Taco Trader
Build a daily American mahjong puzzle app where every answer exports as a 9:16 rack video in the format that wins on Instagram, so playing the app is making content.
Why this app
The most repeatable American mahjong winner on Instagram is one decision on one rack, so the app's core moment is exactly that decision, and each answer comes out as a clip in the same look. The research covered 48 torn-down reels and 2,587 scanned reels from 61 creators (October 2026). "× typical" means times that account's median views.

Product principles
Sharing should be the natural end of every session, not a button people have to be talked into.
1. The answer is the content. Every finished puzzle renders as a 9:16 clip in the winning format: overhead rack, title banner, the player's reasoning as captions.
2. One decision per clip. One moment of the game per puzzle (which three to pass, which section to aim for), because that is what the 25–35× reels teach.
3. Everyone gets the same rack each day. A shared daily deal makes answers comparable, so "you passed the 6s?!" becomes a comment thread.
4. Show the split. After answering, players see what share of everyone passed each tile. Disagreement is what people post about.
5. Faceless by default. No one ever needs to be on camera; the overhead render looks like the reels that did best.
6. Built for creators too. A creator can post the day's rack as their own episode with their voiceover, and viewers can play the same hand from a link.
7. A weekly rhythm with our own names. A themed drop each weekday, under names that belong to the app, not to any creator's series.
The viral loop
The clip is the ad, the shared rack is the landing page, and the streak turns a visitor into a player who solves tomorrow's rack.
MVP features
The first release is the daily rack, the clip export and the answer split; everything else gives people more racks and more reasons to send one.

User flows
A new player should finish a rack and hold a postable clip within 60 seconds of opening the app, before any sign-up.
1. First open
1. The app opens straight on today's rack: "Today's rack: pick your section, pass three." No sign-up wall.
2. Two quick taps: which card they play (this year's or "I'm learning") and how often they play (new, weekly, I teach).
3. The rack shows 13 tiles with a one-tap sort by suit. Card sections show as chips to choose from.
4. They pick a section, drag three tiles to the pass tray and submit.
5. Result screen: their pass next to the suggested one, one line of why per tile, and the split of all players.
6. "Make my clip": a 9:16 preview of their rack, with an optional 20-second voiceover. Share to Reels or TikTok, or save.
7. Only now, "Keep your streak": sign in with Apple or Google.
2. Daily return
1. A push at the time they chose: today's themed drop (for example, Wednesday is Charleston Call).
2. They answer, see the split and their streak goes up.
3. The share prompt appears only on moments worth posting: a 7-day streak, a rare answer ("only 4% passed this"), a perfect week.
3. Arriving from a friend's clip
1. A viewer sees a rack clip in their feed. The end card and caption say "Play this rack", with a link in the poster's bio or sent by comment keyword.
2. The link opens that exact rack in an instant, install-free version (App Clip on iOS, Instant App on Android, or the web).
3. They play it, then see their pass next to the poster's.
4. "Get tomorrow's rack and keep a streak" leads to the install.
4. Game night
1. The host taps "Plan a night", offers two or three dates and shares the link in the group chat.
2. Friends vote from the link without installing; the most-voted date locks and goes into everyone's calendar.
3. On the day, the app sets seats (who is East) and offers a warm-up rack.
4. If someone drops, it suggests 3- or 2-player rules and shows the setup.
5. Afterwards (P2), it offers a recap clip of the night's best hands.
5. Creator episode
1. A creator taps "Post as my episode" on today's rack and records their voiceover over the overhead render.
2. The clip carries their handle and a link that opens the same rack.
3. Their profile in the app counts plays from their link, so they can see what their audience solved.

Roadmap
A phase opens only when the one before it shows people are sharing; if a gate misses, the next step is fixing the clip or the link, not adding features.
Risks and open questions
The biggest risk is the card: the National Mah Jongg League's annual card is copyrighted, so the app must never reproduce it.
Risk
What we know
What we do about it
Card rights
The annual card is protected by copyright (its layout, typography and other expression), while the game and its rules are not (Lerner David, July 2026). I Love Mahj offers its own clearly labelled card for this reason (I Love Mahj)
Never show or copy the card. Puzzles name sections and describe hands in our own words, and players check them against their own card. Get a lawyer's review before launch; ask the League about a licence
Official competition
NMJL Online is the League's official online game. It requires the physical card plus a membership ($4.99 a month or $49.99 a year) and is built for full games
Don't compete on full online games. Win the 2-minute daily decision and the clip, which it doesn't offer
Wrong answers
A bad "suggested pass" kills trust fast, and strong players disagree
Show the split, not one "correct" answer; check suggestions with simulations plus a teacher's review; label it "one strong pass"
Cold start
K of 0.3 means most players still come from content we post
Post the daily rack clip from day one on our own account; invite 10 teaching creators to post racks with creator links
Instagram links
Reels can't carry tappable links
A short web address on the end card, plus comment-a-word DMs (5 of 15 strategy winners already use them)
