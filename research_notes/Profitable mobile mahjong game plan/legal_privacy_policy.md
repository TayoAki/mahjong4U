*Research date: 2026-10-24. This is issue-spotting, not legal advice. Unless marked "primary," sources are law-firm alerts or trade press. The web-search budget ran out before every lead could be checked; the Gaps sections list what remains open.*

## Q1. Gambling-law risk: where does F2P mahjong end and gambling or social casino begin (Apple 5.3, Google Real-Money Gambling, simulated gambling, real-money mahjong by jurisdiction, coin wagering, sweepstakes, cash tournaments, age ratings)?

### Takeaway
A mahjong game is low-risk when currency can't be cashed out, transferred, or staked on match results, and when purchases buy only content or convenience. Risk rises sharply with any of these: purchasable coins wagered on PvP outcomes, tradable chips, sweepstakes dual-currency models, or cash-prize tournaments. Those features bring store-policy limits, gambling-law exposure (US class actions under state "loss recovery" laws, the 2025–26 sweepstakes bans) and higher age ratings (PEGI 18 for games that "closely resemble gambling").

### Cited Findings

**Apple App Store (primary; guidelines page shows "Last Updated: October 16, 2026")**
- 5.3 preamble: "Gaming, gambling, and lotteries can be tricky to manage and tend to be one of the most regulated offerings on the App Store. Only include this functionality if you've fully vetted your legal obligations everywhere you make your app available and are prepared for extra time during the review process." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.3.1: "Sweepstakes and contests must be sponsored by the developer of the app." 5.3.2: "Official rules for sweepstakes, contests, and raffles must be presented in the app and make clear that Apple is not a sponsor or involved in the activity in any manner." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.3.3: "Apps may not use in-app purchase to purchase credit or currency for use in conjunction with real money gaming of any kind." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.3.4: real-money gaming apps (e.g., "sports betting, poker, casino games, horse racing") or lotteries "must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free on the App Store. Illegal gambling aids, including card counters, are not permitted." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Google Play, Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests policy (primary)**
- Real-money gambling apps (online casino, sports betting, lotteries, daily fantasy sports, etc.) are allowed only with approval and a valid license, and may not use Play's in-app billing. Other "real-money games and contests" where users "wager, stake, or participate using real money" are restricted, e.g., "games that accept money and offer prizes of cash or other real world value." — [Google Play Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Apps may not "accept or manage wagers, in-app currencies, winnings, or deposits" to gamble for or win a physical or monetary prize. — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Loyalty programs in game apps: rewards may be awarded and redeemed only at a fixed ratio and may not be "wagered, awarded or exponentiated by game performance or chance-based outcomes". Unlike non-game apps, game apps may not tie loyalty rewards to chance. — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Sweepstakes or drawings must "Specify a fixed number of winners, fixed entry deadline, and prize award date, per promotion," and chance-based programs must disclose odds or selection method in the official rules. — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Gambling ads: an app that shows gambling ads "must not provide simulated gambling content (for example, social casino apps; apps with virtual slot machines)". A simulated casino game may not promote or direct users to real-money casinos. — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Google occasionally runs limited, time-boxed real-money gaming pilots in selected regions. Example: the Japan online crane game pilot ended July 11, 2023, and from July 12, 2023 crane game apps can be listed globally "subject to applicable law and certain requirements." — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)
- Play Billing may not be used to pay for "content or services that facilitate online gambling." — [Google Play Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)

**Coin wagering with purchasable currency: US litigation risk**
- *Kater v. Churchill Downs* (9th Cir., March 28, 2018): Big Fish Casino's virtual chips were a "thing of value" under Washington's gambling statute, which covers "a form of credit and a privilege of playing at a game" (RCW 9.46.0285), so the game could be illegal gambling. This held even though chips couldn't be cashed out, because users need chips to keep playing. The plaintiff could recover "the value of the thing so lost." — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/plaintiff-rolls-a-win-in-ninth-circuit-gambling-op); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/in-game-currency-triggers-state-gambling-laws-rendering-mobile-game-illegal-gambling)
- The Kater plaintiff also alleged a secondary market for chips and a transfer fee charged by the operator. — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/in-game-currency-triggers-state-gambling-laws-rendering-mobile-game-illegal-gambling)

**Sweepstakes (dual-currency) crackdown, 2025–2026**
- California AB 831 was signed October 11, 2025 and took effect January 1, 2026. It bans dual-currency online sweepstakes casinos, and enforcement reaches payment processors, geolocation providers and affiliates. — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/10/14/newsom-signs-california-sweepstakes-ban/); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/california-governor-signs-sweepstakes-gambling-ban-into-law-oct-12-2025)
- New York S5935-A was signed by Gov. Hochul in December 2025; sources disagree on December 5 vs. December 8. It took effect immediately, makes it illegal to operate, promote or support online sweepstakes casinos (including for payment processors, platform providers and media affiliates), and sets fines of $10,000–$100,000 per violation. — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/12/08/new-york-hochul-signs-sweepstakes-ban/); [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2025/12/08/new-york-sweepstakes-casino-ban-hochul-sb5935a/)
- Other 2025 bans reported: Connecticut (SB 1235, June), Montana (SB 555), New Jersey (A5447, August). One source counts nine states with full or partial bans. — [TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/california-becomes-latest-state-to-ban-sweepstakes-casinos/); [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2025/10/13/california-sweepstakes-casino-ban-ab-831/)

**Cash tournaments and skill gaming**
- US states classify skill vs. chance with three tests: "predominant factor" (most common), "material element", and "any chance". One source says Arkansas, Iowa and Tennessee treat a game as gambling if chance plays any role. In predominant-factor states, prizes should be fixed in advance rather than pooled from entry fees, and head-to-head wagers are treated more harshly than tournaments with announced prizes. — [FirstAmendment.com (Walters Law Group)](https://www.firstamendment.com/skill-gaming-states/); [Ifrah Law (2026)](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/gambling-on-skill-enforcement-of-gambling-laws-against-skill-games-in-outlier-states/)
- *Skillz v. Papaya Gaming* (S.D.N.Y. No. 1:24-cv-01646): on April 23, 2026 a jury found Papaya misrepresented its cash games as fair, skill-based human-vs-human competition while secretly using bots, and awarded $420M. On July 27–28, 2026 (sources differ) Judge Cote entered a $719M disgorgement judgment plus about $10M in fees. Injunctive relief was still pending at last report. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/skillz-v-papaya-gaming-record-lanham-act-verdict-puts-bots-matchmaking-and-skill); [PocketGamer.biz](https://pocketgamer.biz/court-orders-papaya-gaming-to-pay-skillz-719m-in-false-advertising-case); [RegulatoryOversight (July 2026)](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/07/when-skill-based-isnt-the-papaya-gaming-verdict-and-its-implications-for-mobile-gaming-operators/)
- Commentary on the verdict argues undisclosed bots could turn a "skill" game into gambling under New York's definition (staking value on an outcome outside the player's control). Liability, however, was under the Lanham Act and NY GBL consumer-protection law, not a gambling finding. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/skillz-v-papaya-gaming-record-lanham-act-verdict-puts-bots-matchmaking-and-skill)

**Real-money mahjong by jurisdiction**
- **China:** reportedly, regulators (then SAPP) stopped approving new poker and mahjong video game licenses over fears they enable illegal gambling (announced April 10; year not confirmed in the sources). The Ministry of Public Security's campaign against cross-border gambling, running since 2020, had shut more than 3,400 gambling platforms by 2021. In 2023, platforms removed "virtual lucky draw" features seen as gambling vectors. — [GGB News](https://ggbnews.com/article/digital-poker-folds-in-china); [AGB (2021)](https://agbrief.com/news/china/09/04/2021/more-than-3400-online-gaming-ops-taken-down-since-2020/); [AGB (2023)](https://agbrief.com/news/27/08/2023/online-gaming-crackdown-forces-chinas-digital-content-platforms-to-remove-live-streaming-features/)
- **Japan:** Penal Code Art. 185 (simple gambling, fine up to ¥500,000) and Art. 186 (habitual gambling, up to 3 years). Playing games of chance for money at online casinos is treated as illegal. — [Monolith Law Office](https://monolith.law/en/general-corporate/online-casino)
- **Hong Kong:** mahjong/tin kau premises are licensed under s.22 of the Gambling Ordinance (Cap. 148) by the Home Affairs Department's Office of the Licensing Authority. Other gambling is illegal unless specifically authorised, and using foreign gambling services is reportedly an offence for persons in Hong Kong. — [HK Office of the Licensing Authority form guide](https://www.hadla.gov.hk/el/filemanager/common/docs/forms/MJ-TK_Application_Forms_Guide_eng.pdf); [Mondaq gaming guide](https://www.mondaq.co.uk:443/Guides/Results/43/197/all/title)

**Age ratings and "simulated gambling"**
- ESRB defines "Simulated Gambling" as "Player can gamble without betting or wagering real cash or currency." Separately, "In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)" (introduced April 2020) is an Interactive Element that does not affect the age rating. — [ESRB ratings guide](https://www.esrb.org/ratings-guide/)
- PEGI's new criteria (announced 2026; reported to apply to games submitted from June 2026): paid random items (loot boxes, card packs, gacha, prize wheels) default to PEGI 16. Social casino and blockchain-item games get PEGI 18, which applies when a game "closely resembles gambling". Daily quests and login streaks get PEGI 7, and losing earned content for not returning gets PEGI 12. — [Ada Derana/AFP](https://adaderana.lk/technology/119714/games-with-loot-boxes-to-get-minimum-16-age-rating-across-europe); [Better Internet for Kids (PEGI, June 2026 PDF)](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/PEGI%2025th%20June.pdf); [Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/european-rating-boards-pegi-will-now-label-games-with-lootboxes-as-pegi-16-1670458)
- Apple's age-rating overhaul: 4+, 9+, 13+, 16+ and 18+ (12+ and 17+ removed). Developers had to answer the new questionnaire by January 31, 2026 or have updates blocked. Ratings can vary by region. — [9to5Mac (July 24, 2025)](https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/24/apple-notifies-developers-of-new-app-store-age-rating-system/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/apple-overhauls-app-store-age-ratings-adds-new-13-16-and-18-categories-486587-2025-07-28)
- Apple 2.3.6: "Answer the age rating questions in App Store Connect honestly so that your app aligns properly with parental controls." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- **Safe design:** solitaire/tile-matching mahjong, or 4-player mahjong scored in points only. Coins used only for cosmetics, hints, undo or shuffles. No cash-out, no player-to-player transfer or gifting, no marketplace. This avoids "real-money gaming" under Apple 5.3 and Google RMG. A "simulated gambling" questionnaire answer is probably unnecessary unless coins are staked on hands.
- **High risk:** buy-in coin tables in multiplayer mahjong, where purchasable coins are staked and the winner takes the loser's coins. This matches the Big Fish pattern in *Kater* (purchasable credits that let you keep playing, staked on chance-influenced outcomes). It invites Washington-style "loss recovery" class actions, likely forces an honest "simulated gambling" answer (higher Apple rating), and could reach PEGI 18 under the 2026 criteria. If used at all: ensure coins can't be bought specifically to stake, regenerate free coins, ban transfers, and get counsel's review.
- **Avoid entirely:** sweepstakes or dual-currency "redeemable coins". They are now banned outright in CA, NY, NJ, CT, MT and others, with enforcement that reaches support vendors. Real-money mahjong is a licensed-gambling business, not an F2P feature, and chance-heavy tile draws make "skill" classification shaky, especially in any-chance states.
- **Later cash tournaments:** only through a reputable skill platform, geo-blocked in any-chance and material-element states, with fixed prizes, no bots (or clearly disclosed bots), and accurate "real players" claims. Papaya shows bots plus "fair human competition" marketing can cost hundreds of millions under false-advertising law alone.
- **Never market to China or Hong Kong as "gambling mahjong."** China's licensing is effectively closed to mahjong/poker titles, and Hong Kong prohibits unlicensed gambling, including use of foreign services.

## Q2. Loot boxes, randomized items and dark patterns (odds disclosure, China, Japan, Belgium/Netherlands, UK, Brazil 2026, FTC Genshin 2025, EU CPC virtual-currency principles 2025)

### Takeaway
Disclosing odds before purchase is mandatory on both stores. Paid random items are banned for minors in Brazil (from March 2026) and treated as illegal gambling in Belgium. They now trigger PEGI 16 by default, and the FTC (Genshin, Jan 2025) and EU CPC (Mar 2025) treat obscured real-money prices and pressure tactics as deceptive. For a small mahjong team, the simplest path is no paid random items at all: sell direct items, bundles, ad removal or a subscription, priced clearly in real currency.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple 3.1.1 (primary):** "Apps offering 'loot boxes' or other mechanisms that provide randomized virtual items for purchase must disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers prior to purchase." Also: "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Google Play Payments §7 (primary):** apps with randomized items "must clearly disclose the odds of receiving those items in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to, that purchase." §5: "In-app virtual currencies must only be used within the app or game title for which they were purchased." — [Google Play Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)
- **FTC v. Cognosphere (HoYoverse, Genshin Impact):** complaint filed January 17, 2025 (C.D. Cal.), settled for $20M. Alleged COPPA violations and deceptive loot-box marketing that misled players about odds and real costs. The order bars loot-box sales to under-16s without parental consent and requires correcting misrepresentations of win chances and prices. Reports say it requires showing odds and prices in real-money terms. — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/gaming/ftc-cracks-down-on-genshin-impact-gacha-loot-box-practices); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/genshin-impact-maker-fined-20m-over-deceptive-loot-boxes-and-child-privacy-violation); [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/)
- **EU CPC Network "Key Principles on In-Game Virtual Currencies" (March 21, 2025):** currency that primarily works as payment is treated as representing real money, so both buying and spending it fall under EU consumer law. The principles call for clear prices and pre-contract information, no hidden costs, no forcing players to buy more currency than needed, respect for withdrawal rights, and protection of vulnerable users, especially children. They are non-binding, but issued alongside coordinated action against Star Stable. One firm notes that under the wider consumer-law framework, non-compliance can bring fines of up to EUR 800,000 or 6% of total annual turnover (the figure appears in a Belgium-focused analysis). — [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/european-consumer-protection-network-issues-new-key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies-impact-for-gaming-and-gambling-entities-in-belgium-the-eu-and-beyond/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/qas-on-the-eu-consumer-protection-authorities-joint-guidance-paper/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/level-up-compliance-video-games-industry-in-the-spotlight-again-for-consumer-law-compliance)
- **Brazil, Law 15.211/2025 ("ECA Digital"):** enacted September 17, 2025, in force March 2026 (most sources say March 17, 2026). "The use of reward boxes (loot boxes) in electronic games aimed at children and adolescents is strictly prohibited," covering games "intended for or likely to be accessed by minors." Age verification must be "reliable and auditable", and self-declaration is not accepted. Accounts of users up to 16 must be linked to a guardian. Fines go up to 10% of Brazilian gross revenue, or BRL 10–1,000 per registered user capped at BRL 50M per violation. — [Demarest (Sept 19, 2025)](https://www.demarest.com.br/en/eca-digital-nova-lei-de-protecao-de-criancas-e-adolescentes-no-ambiente-digital/)
- Brazil enforcement: the ANPD reportedly runs a two-phase approach, app stores and OSes first, then game studios from August 2026. One source says the ban is effectively 18+, which conflicts with the "respecting age rating" wording elsewhere. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/legal/item-48938); [Unico](https://www.unico.io/releases/navigating-the-digital-eca-compliance-as-a-growth-driver-in-gaming)
- **Belgium:** the Gaming Commission concluded in 2018 that paid loot boxes are illegal gambling. Criminal fines reach €800,000, and a 2025 Antwerp ruling is cited as treating paid loot boxes as unlawful. The regulator admits monitoring is difficult. — [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/direction-of-travel-for-loot-boxes-in-the-eu)
- **Netherlands:** a March 2022 court decision overturned the gambling authority's €10M fine against EA, so paid loot boxes are now generally lawful. Transferable or cash-out contents remain the key risk factor. Sources conflict somewhat. — [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/direction-of-travel-for-loot-boxes-in-the-eu)
- **UK:** no statute. Ukie's 11 industry principles (July 2023, from a DCMS working group) include technical controls so under-18s can't buy loot boxes without parental consent, disclosure of loot boxes before purchase or download, probability disclosure, refunds for unauthorised purchases by minors, and action against real-money resale. — [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/esports/video-gaming/uk-games-industry-commits-to-limiting-under-18s-loot-box-access/); [Digit.fyi](https://www.digit.fyi/ukie-publishes-loot-box-principles-for-uk-video-games-industry/)
- **Japan:** "kompu gacha" (collect a set of random items to unlock a reward) was ruled a violation of the Premiums and Representations Act by the Consumer Affairs Agency in May 2012, enforced from July 2012. Ordinary gacha remains legal. Odds disclosure comes from industry guidelines (JOGA 2012, JASGA 2013, CESA 2016), which mainly bind members. — [Monolith Law](https://monolith.law/en/general-corporate/game-random-complete-illegal); [DiGRA paper](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2891/2875/2934)
- **China:** since 2017, publishers must disclose possible loot-box rewards and their probabilities. — [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/)
- **PEGI (2026):** paid random items default to PEGI 16. Login streaks and daily quests get PEGI 7, and mechanics that take away earned content for not returning get PEGI 12. — [Ada Derana/AFP](https://adaderana.lk/technology/119714/games-with-loot-boxes-to-get-minimum-16-age-rating-across-europe)

### Inferences
- **Recommended monetization:** remove-ads IAP, hint/undo/shuffle packs at fixed prices, cosmetic tile/background themes sold directly, battle pass/season pass with visible rewards, an optional subscription. This sidesteps loot-box law almost everywhere and avoids PEGI 16.
- **If any randomness is monetized** (e.g., "mystery tile set" chests): disclose odds per item before purchase, show prices in local currency next to virtual-currency prices, never let currency or items be transferred or cashed out, block under-18s (UK principles) or under-16s without parental consent (FTC Genshin), geo-disable in Belgium, and disable for minors in Brazil.
- **Watch the dark-pattern list.** Daily streaks that punish absence, countdown "limited offers", and currency packs that leave unusable leftovers raise PEGI ratings and draw CPC/FTC scrutiny. Retention streaks are common in mahjong solitaire games, so design them as rewards rather than penalties.
- Purchased currency must never expire (Apple 3.1.1), and IAP restore must work.

- Primary text of the FTC Genshin final order (the FTC press-release URL returned 404) and whether the court entered it: not verified.
- Exact Brazilian article number and age threshold (rating-based vs. 18+): sources conflict.
- No 2025 DCMS evaluation of the Ukie principles found.
- The EU Digital Fairness Act proposal (expected to address virtual currencies and dark patterns): not researched this session.

## Q3. Privacy: ATT, nutrition labels, privacy manifests, SKAdNetwork/AdAttributionKit, Google Data safety, GDPR consent and Google's certified CMP, US state laws, COPPA 2025 amendments, child-directed status, app-store age-assurance laws

### Takeaway
An ad-funded mahjong game needs four things to launch: ATT done correctly with no gating or incentives, accurate nutrition labels and Data safety forms that cover every SDK, privacy manifests for required-reason APIs and listed SDKs, and a Google-certified TCF CMP for EEA/UK/CH traffic (without it there are no personalized AdMob ads). In 2026 the growth area is age assurance. Texas SB 2420 is enforceable (since the May 28, 2026 Fifth Circuit stay), California AB 1043 starts January 1, 2027, and Utah, Louisiana and Alabama follow in 2027. Developers must consume Apple's Declared Age Range and Google's Play Age Signals APIs and treat the signal as actual knowledge of age. That makes COPPA's amended rule (compliance from April 22, 2026) directly relevant even for a general-audience game.

### Cited Findings

**Apple (primary)**
- Tracking "refers to the act of linking user or device data collected from your app with user or device data collected from other companies' apps, websites, or offline properties for targeted advertising or advertising measurement purposes... [and] sharing user or device data with data brokers." Apps need ATT permission to track or read the IDFA. Without it, the IDFA is all zeros and other identifiers may not be used to track. — [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)
- No gating or incentives: "Can I gate functionality on agreeing to allow tracking, or incentivize users to agree...? No, per the App Review Guidelines: 5.1.2(i)." Fingerprinting is barred: "you may not derive data from a device for the purpose of uniquely identifying it." — [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)
- Guideline 5.1.2: "You must receive explicit permission from users via the App Tracking Transparency APIs to track their activity"; apps "may not require users to enable system functionalities (such as push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality, content, use the app, or receive monetary or other compensation." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Nutrition labels: "If your app uses third-party code, such as advertising or analytics SDKs, you must describe what data it collects, how it is used, and whether it tracks users." "Developers are responsible for all code included in their apps." — [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)
- Attribution: Apple's page now points to AdAttributionKit for privacy-preserving ad attribution and doesn't mention SKAdNetwork. It also describes an EU alternative ATT prompt ("Additional Information" button) starting iOS/iPadOS 27.2, which will be the only version in Germany, France, Italy, Poland and Romania, with re-prompting allowed after one year. — [Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)
- Privacy manifests: since May 1, 2024, App Store Connect rejects new apps and updates that don't declare required-reason API use (file timestamp, system boot time, disk space, active keyboard, user defaults). Newly added SDKs on Apple's commonly-used list need their own privacy manifest and, for binary dependencies, valid signatures. — [Bitrise](https://bitrise.io/blog/post/enforcement-of-apple-privacy-manifest-starting-from-may-1-2024); [Adjust help](https://help.adjust.com/en/article/submit-privacy-details-to-apple)
- Account deletion, 5.1.1(v): "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app," and "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Google Play (primary)**
- Data safety: "All developers that have an app published on Google Play must complete the Data safety form," including apps that collect nothing (a privacy policy link is still required). Developers "must reflect data collection or sharing carried out by such third-party code." "You alone are responsible for making complete and accurate declarations." Misrepresentation leads to "policy enforcement, like blocked updates or removal from Google Play." — [Google Play Data safety help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)
- Account deletion: apps that allow account creation must offer in-app deletion of the account and its data plus a web link to request deletion, declared in the Data safety form. Deactivation doesn't count. Original deadlines: Dec 7, 2023, extendable to May 31, 2024. — [Google Play account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-data-safety-3309792/)

**GDPR/UK GDPR consent and Google's CMP mandate**
- Publishers using AdMob, AdSense or Ad Manager must use a Google-certified CMP integrated with IAB TCF to serve personalized ads: in the EEA and UK since January 16, 2024, and in Switzerland since July 31, 2024. Otherwise "you will not be eligible to serve personalized ads." ### Inferences
- **Child-directed status:** position the game as general audience (adult mahjong players). Use adult-oriented art and marketing, avoid cartoon mascots and kid-themed tile sets, keep it out of Apple's Kids Category and Google's child-only "Target audience" settings, and don't buy ads aimed at children. Age-signal laws are eroding the "we don't know who's a kid" position, though. Once Apple or Google return an under-13 signal (AB 1043 deems this actual knowledge; Texas requires using it), that user must get COPPA treatment: no behavioral ads, contextual ads only through Families-certified SDKs, and no data sharing. Build an "age-band router" now: 18+ gets full ATT/CMP-gated ads and IAP; 13–17 gets non-personalized ads, no random paid items, and spending guardrails; under 13 gets contextual or no ads, no third-party analytics IDs, and parent-gated purchases.
- **Ad SDK hygiene:** pick an AdMob/AppLovin/ironSource stack with current privacy manifests and signatures. Map every SDK's data flows into both the nutrition label and the Data safety form, since inaccuracies lead to blocked updates or removal. Turn on the TCF CMP before first ad request in the EEA/UK/CH. Respect ATT denial, with no fingerprinting through MMPs.
- **Accounts:** if offering cloud save or login, ship in-app deletion (Apple and Google) plus a web deletion URL (Google). Consider guest play by default, which Apple 5.1.1(v) encourages.
- **SB 2420 "significant change" notices:** adding IAP, new data uses or rating changes after launch may need store notification and parental re-consent for Texas minors, so plan release notes and compliance tickets accordingly.

## Q4. Ads policy: Google Play disruptive and full-screen ad rules, Apple ad rules, family/children policies, misleading ad creatives (UK ASA, Meta)

### Takeaway
Interstitial placement is a common cause of rejection and removal for casual games. Google bans unexpected interstitials, interstitials at the start of a level or before the splash screen, and any not closeable after 15 seconds. Apple requires clear close buttons and in-app reporting of inappropriate ads. Ads must suit the app's age rating, and any users who may be children need Families self-certified SDKs. In UA creative, the UK ASA treats ads showing gameplay that isn't the core experience as misleading, and disclaimers don't fix that.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple 2.5.18 (primary):** ads only in the main binary (not in extensions, widgets or notifications); "must be appropriate for the app's age rating"; users must be able to see the targeting data used; no behavioral ads based on sensitive data or "from kids (such as from apps in the App Store's Kids Category)"; "Interstitial ads or ads that interrupt or block the user experience must clearly indicate that they are an ad, must not manipulate or trick users into tapping into them, and must provide easily accessible and visible close/skip buttons large enough for people to easily dismiss the ad. Apps that contain ads must also include the ability for users to report any inappropriate or age-inappropriate ads." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 1.3 Kids Category (primary):** no third-party ads or analytics, except, in limited cases, contextual ads from services with documented Kids practices "that include human review of ad creatives for age appropriateness." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Google Play Ads policy (primary):**
  - Disallowed: "Full screen interstitial ads of all formats (video, GIF, static, etc.) that show unexpectedly"; "Ads that appear during game play at the beginning of a level or during the beginning of a content segment"; "Full screen video interstitial ads that appear before an app's loading screen (splash screen)"; and "Full screen interstitial ads of all formats that are not closeable after 15 seconds."
  - Exempt: opt-in interstitials and rewarded ads.
  - Apps also may not force ad clicks, and ads must be "easily dismissible without penalty" and must not mimic app UI or system notifications.
  - The rules are based on the Coalition for Better Ads standards.
  — [Google Play Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753)
- **Google Families ads (primary):** apps targeting only children must use ads SDK versions self-certified for Families. In mixed-audience apps, ads shown to children must come exclusively from self-certified SDKs (e.g., through a neutral age screen). Mediation for child or unknown-age requests should use child treatment and max ad content rating G. — [Google Play Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12918983); [AdMob help](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6223431)
- **UK ASA, Top Games Inc (Evony), ruling 3 April 2024:** a paid X ad breached CAP Code rule 3.1. Although the puzzle footage was "comparable" to real gameplay, "Evony was not primarily a puzzle game," and "the ad did not reflect the game's core playing experience and was therefore misleading." — [ASA ruling](https://asa.org.uk/rulings/top-games-inc-a23-1213924-top-games-inc.html)
- **UK ASA, Playrix (Homescapes/Gardenscapes):** Facebook ads showed mini-games that only about 0.03% of players reached. Breaches of CAP 3.1, 3.9 and 3.11; the "not all images represent actual gameplay" disclaimer did not cure them. — [TechRaptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/news/uk-asa-upholds-misleading-pin-pulling-puzzle-ad-claims-against-playrix); [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/homescapes-gardenscapes-ads-banned-by-asa-for-misrepresentation/)
- **Simulated-gambling apps** may not show real-money gambling ads on Google Play. — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)

### Inferences
- **Mahjong ad placement:** interstitials only at natural breaks (after a solved board or results screen), never at level start, never before the splash screen, close button within ≤15s (better ≤5s), plus rewarded video for hints, undo or shuffle. Add an in-app "report this ad" control (Apple). Set ad-network content ratings to match the store rating, and block gambling and adult categories, especially if under-18 signals are present.
- **UA creative:** show real mahjong gameplay. Avoid "fail" bait or fake mini-game footage, "win cash" or "earn money" claims (which also trigger gambling and sweepstakes concerns), and fake price drops. Keep footage representative of the core loop (ASA test). Never imply real-money prizes for a non-cash game.

- Meta's ad policy text on misleading game ads and gambling-adjacent creative: not researched (budget).
- No mahjong-specific ASA ruling found. No 2025–2026 ASA game-ad rulings verified beyond those above.
- FTC actions on fake-gameplay mobile ads in the US: not found.

## Q5. Intellectual property: is "Mahjong" generic; trademark conflicts (Mahjong Solitaire, Shanghai/Activision, Vita, Mah Jongg/NMJL); NMJL card hands; tile art and layouts; clones and store takedowns

### Takeaway
"Mahjong/Mah Jongg" is a generic game name: USPTO filings routinely disclaim it, and game rules and winning-hand concepts aren't copyrightable. Copyright and trademark risk sits in expression and branding: the NMJL card's layout, typography and formatting; tile artwork; distinctive names like "Shanghai" (Activision's registration lapsed in 2020, but check common-law and other filings); and copying a competitor's UI or presentation (Tetris v. Xio; Apple 4.1/4.3). Using the NMJL's annual card hands in an American Mahjong app is a gray area. The hands themselves are arguably unprotectable, but reproducing the card's presentation or marketing it as "the NMJL card" is risky, and the NMJL sells its own official app.

### Cited Findings
- US copyright excludes "ideas, procedures, systems, and methods of operation" (17 U.S.C. §102(b)), so the rules of American Mah Jongg and the concept of winning hands are free to use. Tile manufacturers own their tile art, by analogy to playing-card makers owning card art but not poker. The NMJL annual card ("Official Standard Hands and Rules") is protected as an original work, but the protection covers expression (layout, typography, colors, formatting), "not the winning hands listed." Scanning, copying or distributing the card is the infringing act. *(Lerner David LLP, July 2026, written by a summer associate; no cases or enforcement cited.)* — [Lerner David](https://www.lernerdavid.com/news-successes/unscripted/copyright-issues-in-america-s-fast-growing-mah-jongg-craze)
- The NMJL sells a new card each year (2026 card offered; site footer "© THE NATIONAL MAH JONGG LEAGUE, INC.") and runs "NMJL Online", with an iPad/iPhone version, as its official online game. Access requires the annual card plus a membership. — [NMJL website](https://www.nationalmahjonggleague.org/); [NMJL Online, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nmjl-online/id6476651173)
- The I Love Mahj app says the NMJL card is "copyrighted material, so there's no way we (or anyone else for that matter) could be offering free downloads of the card," and offers its own alternative card instead. — [I Love Mahj knowledge base](https://ilovemahj.com/kb/67000755029)
- Third-party app Mahjong Mate matches a player's tiles against the lines on the NMJL card. Whether it is licensed is unverified. — [Mahjong Mate, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6478509695)
- USPTO records (via a third-party aggregator) show "MAHJONG"/"MAHJONGG" disclaimed in marks such as GALAXY OF MAHJONGG (cancelled 2021), AMERICAN MAHJONG and MY MAHJONG SECRETARY. The bare word can't be owned exclusively, but composite marks can be registered. — [trademarkregistration.app records](https://trademarkregistration.app/Trademark/Details/75322329)
- SHANGHAI (Reg. No. 1,904,528, Serial 74468336), owned by Activision Publishing: filed December 10, 1993, registered July 11, 1995, cancelled April 3, 2020 for failure to file the §8 declaration. SHANGHAI DYNASTY (Reg. 2,462,564) was cancelled January 20, 2012. — [trademarkregistration.app, SHANGHAI](https://trademarkregistration.app/Trademark/Details/74468336); [Justia, SHANGHAI: DYNASTY](https://trademark.justia.com/752/90/shanghai-dynasty-75290997.html)
- *Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive*, 863 F. Supp. 2d 394 (D.N.J. May 30, 2012): rules and mechanics aren't protectable, but the iPhone clone Mino infringed by copying expressive choices (10×20 board, ghost piece look, colors). Merger and scènes à faire defenses were rejected. It is a district-court decision that turned on admitted deliberate copying. — [Willamette IP summary](https://wlo.willamette.edu/ip/2012/05/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc.html); [Cole Schotz](https://coleschotz.com/tetris-defeats-the-clones-in-copyright-infringement-battle)
- **Apple 4.1 Copycats (primary):** "Don't simply copy the latest popular app... or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI and pass it off as your own." "Submitting apps which impersonate other apps or services... may result in removal from the Apple Developer Program." "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 4.3 Spam (primary):** "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available." Saturated categories need "a meaningfully different or improved experience." Multiple bundle IDs of the same app (e.g., reskins) are barred. — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- **Naming:** "Mahjong" plus a distinctive coined word (e.g., "Mahjong [BrandWord]") is generally safe. Avoid names close to established titles (e.g., "Vita Mahjong", "Mahjong Solitaire: [competitor's subtitle]", "Shanghai" as a title or ASO keyword) and avoid competitor names in keyword fields or the icon (Apple 4.1(c)). Do a clearance search (USPTO plus EUIPO and key markets) before launch, and consider filing your own composite mark with "MAHJONG" disclaimed.
- **Tile art and layouts:** commission original tile art (don't trace Unicode glyphs or other apps' sets; check font licenses). Classic arrangements like "Turtle", "Dragon" or "Fortress" are long-standing traditional layouts and arguably unprotectable as arrangements of a functional game. Still, design your own level set and don't copy a competitor's particular level packs, UI chrome, animations or color schemes (the Tetris v. Xio risk). Reskinning one engine into many near-identical apps risks Apple 4.3(a) and 4.3(b) rejection.
- **American Mahjong/NMJL:** lowest-risk options are (a) your own original annual hand card (as I Love Mahj does), (b) a license from the NMJL, or (c) a "bring your own card" mode where users enter hands they own, with no reproduction of NMJL layout or branding. Don't call it "official", don't use NMJL marks or trade dress, and don't reproduce the card's look. Avoid copying all hands verbatim each year: it's legally gray, and the NMJL's commercial stake makes a dispute plausible even if the hands are arguably unprotectable.
- **Clone defense:** keep dated design docs and source repos to prove originality. Use Apple's and Google's IP complaint channels against copycats of your own app.

- NMJL's own licensing terms, its terms of use for the card, and any enforcement history against apps: none found. Real Mah Jongg's licensing relationship with the NMJL: not found.
- No case law found specifically on copyright in mahjong solitaire layouts.
- Unicode/emoji mahjong glyph and font licensing: not researched.

## Q6. Business and tax basics: Small Business Programs (15%), DSA trader status, VAT, entity formation, required privacy policy/terms/EULA, account deletion

### Takeaway
Enroll in both 15% programs. Declare EU DSA trader status, since any monetized developer is effectively a trader and your address, phone and email will be shown publicly to EU users. Apple has removed EU listings lacking trader status since February 17, 2025. Publish a privacy policy (required even for no-data apps on Google Play) and terms. Ship account deletion if you have accounts. Purchased currency must never expire, and restores must work.

## Objective synthesis: prioritized checklist of what could get the app rejected, removed, fined or sued

### Takeaway
For a straightforward F2P mahjong game, the largest practical risks are store rejection or removal from privacy, ad-placement, copycat or DSA lapses. The largest legal risks come from monetization choices: coin wagering, sweepstakes, cash tournaments, paid random items, and children's data. Simple design choices remove most of the second group.

### Cited Findings
- **Rejection/removal triggers** (sources in Q3–Q6): missing or inaccurate privacy manifests or required-reason declarations (rejected since May 1, 2024 — [Bitrise](https://bitrise.io/blog/post/enforcement-of-apple-privacy-manifest-starting-from-may-1-2024)); inaccurate Data safety forms (blocked updates or removal — [Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469)); no in-app account deletion ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)); gating features on ATT ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)); disruptive interstitials ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753), [Apple 2.5.18](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)); copycat or spam reskins ([Apple 4.1/4.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)); missing DSA trader status (EU removal since Feb 17, 2025 — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/?p=55860)); missing odds disclosure for random paid items ([Apple 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Google §7](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)); real-money features without licenses or geo-restriction ([Apple 5.3.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Google RMG](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)); age-rating questionnaire not completed (Apple blocked updates after Jan 31, 2026 — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/24/apple-notifies-developers-of-new-app-store-age-rating-system/)).

### Inferences
**Launch checklist for a small team (ordered by risk reduction per unit of effort):**
1. **Monetization design:** ads + direct-purchase IAP (remove ads, hints, undo, themes, season pass). No paid loot boxes. No coin wagering between players. No cash-out, transfer or gifting of currency. No sweepstakes. No cash tournaments at launch. Purchased currency never expires; restore purchases works.
2. **Age and child posture:** general-audience positioning and art. Answer the Apple/IARC questionnaires honestly (including "simulated gambling" if any coin staking exists). Integrate Apple's Declared Age Range and Google's Play Age Signals APIs. Route under-13 users to COPPA-safe ad settings (contextual ads, Families-certified SDKs, max content rating G, no IDFA/AAID sharing) and under-18 users to non-personalized ads and no random paid items. Log store "significant change" notifications for Texas (now) and Utah/Louisiana/Alabama/California (2027).
3. **Privacy plumbing:** ATT prompt with no incentive or gating, and plan for the EU alternative prompt in iOS 27.2. Google-certified TCF CMP for EEA/UK/CH. CCPA/state opt-out ("Do Not Sell or Share" and GPC). Privacy manifests for the app and every SDK. Matching nutrition label and Data safety form. Privacy policy URL. In-app plus web account deletion if accounts exist.
4. **Ad placement:** interstitials only at natural breaks, never at level start or before the splash, closeable within 15s, rewarded ads opt-in only, in-app "report ad" control, gambling and adult ad categories blocked.
5. **UA creative:** real core gameplay only. No "earn cash" claims. Avoid ASA-style fake mini-game ads.
6. **IP:** original name (clearance search; "MAHJONG" disclaimed), original tile art and level designs, no "Shanghai" or competitor names in titles or keywords, no NMJL card reproduction or branding (own card, license, or BYO-card mode), keep originality records.
7. **Business:** form an entity, enroll in Apple and Google 15% programs, complete Apple and Google DSA trader declarations with a business address, publish ToS/EULA (virtual items are a licence with no cash value) and official rules for any contests.

### Gaps
- No single primary source compiles these items. The checklist is a synthesis of the sources above and should be reviewed by gaming/privacy counsel before launch, especially for any multiplayer coin-staking or tournament feature and for the state age-assurance laws, whose litigation and effective dates were still moving as of October 2026.
