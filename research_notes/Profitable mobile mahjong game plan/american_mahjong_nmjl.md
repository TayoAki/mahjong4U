# American Mahjong (NMJL-card style) App Opportunity: Card Licensing and Competitive Set (as of 2026-10-24)

Method note: App Store figures (rating average and count, first-release date, seller) come from Apple's iTunes Search API, pulled 2026-10-24 for the US storefront. Review themes come from Apple's public customer-review RSS feed (most recent roughly 40–150 reviews per app). Google Play install bands were parsed from public Play listings the same day. ## Q1. NMJL card rights: copyright and trademark claims, licensing, enforcement, commentary, and app workarounds

### Takeaway
The NMJL publicly claims copyright in its content *and in its "selection, coordination, compilation" ("Arrangement")*. It also claims "National Mah Jongg League", "NMJL Online", its logos and its trade dress as trademarks, and it now sells a competing official app (iOS since March 2026). That app requires the physical card, and the League says "the card is not available in electronic form." I found **no public NMJL licensing program, no licensed third-party app, and no reported lawsuits, cease-and-desist letters or app takedowns**. Meanwhile, roughly 20+ third-party apps openly play the current NMJL hands, and several display the full card in the app. Commentators split: the protection covers only layout and typography, or the hands as a curated compilation are protected too. The practical risk is a DMCA or App Store complaint, not a court ruling.

**Enforcement history**
- IP lawyer blog (TMBTQ Law, May 14 2025): "the National Mah Jongg League… holds registered copyrights on its annual card," including 2025, with registrations "going back to 1982." It adds: "**There are no widely reported lawsuits, but enforcement is still possible.**" No registration numbers are given. It also claims statutory damages "can exceed $25,000 per violation" without a citation. — [TMBTQ Law](https://www.tmbtq.com/post/ip-and-mah-jongg/). The statute sets $750–$30,000 per work infringed, up to $150,000 if willful; statutory damages also generally require timely registration, which the NMJL reportedly has. — [17 U.S.C. §504(c), Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/504)
- My searches for NMJL cease-and-desist letters, app takedowns, or Reddit reports of removed apps returned nothing specific to the NMJL. — (negative result; see Gaps)
- The visible enforcement concern in the community is **counterfeit physical cards** sold on Amazon and Temu ("can look just like the real thing but contain mistakes"). — [Tom Sloper, Mah-Jongg FAQ 7i (2023)](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/cards.html); [Mahjongg blog](http://mahjblog.blogspot.com/2015/03/copying-card-dont.html)
- Platform mechanics: Apple accepts copyright complaints through a form and may remove an app while a claim is reviewed. Fair use is not guaranteed to be honored at the platform level. — [AppleInsider, Nov 2024](https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/11/20/apples-quick-app-store-takedowns-over-copyright-claims-are-a-nightmare-for-developers)

**Lawyer and commentator views (conflicting)**
- Lerner David LLP (July 2026, by summer associate Hayley Yang, edited by Craig Drachtman; syndicated on Mondaq): "the official annual card is protected by copyright." The protection covers original expression such as "layout, typography, color, and formatting," **not the underlying game or rules, including which hands win in a given year**. Scanning, photocopying or distributing copies is the restricted act. It cites only 17 U.S.C. §102(b), with no cases and nothing on apps or trademarks. — [Lerner David](https://www.lernerdavid.com/news-successes/unscripted/copyright-issues-in-america-s-fast-growing-mah-jongg-craze); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/copyright/1825446/copyright-issues-in-americas-fast-growing-mah-jongg-craze)
- TMBTQ Law (2025): lists of names or numbers and methods of play are not copyrightable, but "written rules, designs, and layouts can qualify." Unofficial cards that only change layout, fonts or colors "may not avoid infringement claims." A self-designed card should be "clearly different." — [TMBTQ Law](https://www.tmbtq.com/post/ip-and-mah-jongg/)
- Mahj Life instructor wiki (Apr 2025, updated Aug 2025; unsigned, cites no authority): "The National Mah Jongg League owns the copyright to the annual card, including its content, layout, and format… **Reproducing hands, even in part, for distribution (printed or digital) is a copyright violation.**" It lists "creating digital replicas of hands" as not covered by fair use. This contradicts Lerner David on whether hands are protected. — [Mahj Life](https://mahjlife.com/wiki/copyright-fair-use-article-256/)
- I Love Mahj (a web competitor) says the NMJL card is "copyrighted material, so there's no way we (or anyone else for that matter) could be offering free downloads of the card." It offers its own free alternative card. — [I Love Mahj KB](https://ilovemahj.com/kb/67000755029)

### Inferences
- **Two legal theories collide.** (a) Under §102(b), winning hands are game rules or a system, so an engine implementing them is fine (the Lerner David view). (b) The NMJL claims copyright in the *selection and arrangement*, a compilation theory, so reproducing the full set of 50–60 lines each year, even in a new layout, copies the protectable selection (the Mahj Life and TMBTQ view). No court ruling was found. The safest reading is that displaying or listing all of the year's hands as a card substitute carries the most risk. Validating hands behind the scenes, with the user holding their own card, carries less.
- **Contract risk is separate from copyright risk.** The "no software development" clause binds people who accept the NMJL Online ToS. A team should never use NMJL Online (or its data) to build an engine, and should source hands only from a purchased physical card or not at all.
- **Enforcement motive rose in 2026.** The League shipped a paid iOS app in March 2026 and revised its ToS (trade names and logos) in February 2026. Before that, many apps coexisted with no visible enforcement. A League with its own app is more likely to file App Store or DMCA complaints against competitors that market "NMJL card" play or show the card. Apple can pull an app during review even if the use is defensible.
- **Trademark hygiene matters more than the hands question for marketing.** Never use "National Mah Jongg League", "NMJL" or "official" in an app name, icon or keyword-stuffed title, and never use the card's look (colors and layout). Compatibility statements with disclaimers ("works with your NMJL card; not affiliated") appear to be common practice, and nominative fair use may cover them. Real Mah Jongg's "official card" and MahJongg4Fun's "Official Online American Mah Jongg App" wording looks risky.
- **The best-supported strategies for a new entrant,** from lowest to highest IP risk: (1) an original house card or a license from an alternative-card publisher (Marvelous, Mahjong Press, Big Card, AMJA or AMS), plus (2) a "bring your own card" mode where the user owns the card and the app validates against user-entered or locally stored patterns, plus (3) asking the NMJL directly for a license. The NMJL has never been shown to license, and as a not-for-profit with its own app, a "no" is plausible. A fourth option, embedding the current NMJL hands like ~20 competitors do, is common but uncleared. It should be a deliberate, counsel-reviewed choice with a takedown contingency.

### Gaps
- No NMJL statement on third-party apps, digital rights or licensing terms was found. The FAQ is empty, and no press interview with NMJL leadership on apps turned up.
- Copyright registration numbers and whether registrations cover the card as a compilation were not checked. Next step: the U.S. Copyright Office Public Catalog, claimant "National Mah Jongg League."
- No primary USPTO TSDR verification of Reg. 3,249,385 or of any "NMJL"/"NMJL ONLINE" applications (aggregator only).
- No evidence either way of private cease-and-desist letters. Absence of public reports does not mean absence of letters. Direct outreach to app founders (e.g., Real Mah Jongg, operating since 2017) could clarify.
- The NMJL Online web launch date and Quicksilver's contract terms are not documented publicly. The iOS release date (2026-03-11) is confirmed. The App Store ID (6476651173) suggests the listing record was created around early 2024; that is an inference from ID sequencing.

## Q2. Competitive set: platforms, business model and pricing, card approach, features, ratings and installs

### Takeaway
The category is crowded and fragmenting fast. Among apps surfaced by an App Store keyword search, roughly 25 American-mahjong-oriented iOS apps first launched in 2026. Scale still sits with four incumbents: Mahjong 4 Friends (25.6K ratings), American Mahjong Practice / Eight Bam (22.9K), The Mahjong App (7.9K, launched only 2026-05-01) and Real Mah Jongg (5.7K). The standard model is a subscription at $3.99–$6.99/mo or $17.99–$59.99/yr, with ad-supported freemium (M4F) and one-time unlocks (Eight Bam, MAHJ) as variants. Bots, private friend tables, Charleston support, suggested hands and tutorials are now table stakes. The official NMJL app is negligible (4 ratings).

### Inferences
- **The window is narrowing but still open.** The Mahjong App reached about 7.9K ratings in under six months, which shows a well-executed newcomer can overtake 2017–2021 incumbents quickly. Real Mah Jongg (no update since April 2026) and M4F (a broken update in October 2026) look vulnerable. About 25 launches in 2026 means app-store differentiation, community distribution and quality now decide outcomes.
- **iOS dominates.** Eight Bam has 22.9K iOS ratings against 113 Google Play reviews (10K+ installs), and Real Mah Jongg has 5.7K iOS ratings against 819 GP reviews. This fits an affluent, iPhone and iPad-heavy female 35–70 audience, so iPad support matters. Ship iOS-first with iPad layouts, and add Android later or through cross-platform tooling.
- **Price anchors** are $4.99–$6.99/mo, about $50–60/yr, and $15–18 for a one-time annual-card unlock. A $149.99 lifetime tier exists (The Mahjong App). A free daily game plus a paid unlimited tier is the emerging freemium pattern (Daily Mahj, Maylee, Mahj Parlour).
- **Revenue estimates:** not available from analytics firms. As a rough illustration only: if Eight Bam's 22.9K US ratings correspond to roughly 0.5–1.5M lifetime installs at a 1:25–1:65 ratio, and 3–8% convert at about $18–48/yr, annual revenue would be in the low-to-mid single-digit $ millions at most. Treat this as an order of magnitude, not a finding.

## Q3. Demand evidence: size and growth of the US scene, and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Offline demand is large and well documented, though rarely with audited numbers. The NMJL sells an estimated ~500K–750K cards a year (a 2024 retailer estimate) and has 350K+ members. Facebook groups top 100K members, teen and camp adoption is spreading, and Chinese factories have retooled for American sets. Players demonstrably pay $5–7/mo or $50–70/yr and often call it "worth it" next to studio fees. The main complaints are hard paywalls before learning, trial-cancellation friction, and repaying for each new card year.

## Q4. Product opportunities: features players ask for, and adjacent revenue

### Takeaway
Reviews converge on six fixable pains: bots that feel unrealistic, rule-engine errors on the new card, weak onboarding, rigid tile handling and layout, poor accessibility (small tiles, low contrast) and stability. Remote play with an existing group is the emotional core. Adjacent money is in club, event and score SaaS (with payment take-rates), designer tile-set cosmetics, lessons, and rating systems.

### Cited Findings
- **Bot realism:** "I haven't won once… in the last two pay cycles"; "all five in a row ended as wall games. In real life games, I'd say we only wall about 5% of the time"; "too many jokers thrown away in a row - not realistic"; "deals the same hands over and over" (Real Mah Jongg). ## Q5. Wright-Patterson and other American variants; viability of a card-agnostic "enter your own hands" engine

### Takeaway
Many American-style hand sets exist besides the NMJL card: Marvelous, the Big Card, the International (Oh My Mahjong), Mahjong Press, AMJA and AMS cards, I Love Mahj, Four Friends, Siamese (a separate two-player game) and Wright-Patterson (a rules booklet). Multi-card support is already common in incumbents. A pattern-driven, card-agnostic engine is technically viable and strategically useful, because it can license or host alternative cards and a user's own card. But hand entry is hard for users: one NMJL card's 55 lines expand to about 1,059 concrete hands, and card rule variants differ (joker rules, quints). It works best as a hybrid rather than relying on users typing in a card.

### Cited Findings
- **Alternative cards (Tom Sloper's FAQ, last cleaned Jan 2023):**
  - Marvelous Mah Jongg (Houston): "played using NMJL rules, and comes out in January."
  - The Big Card from The Mahjong Line, issued each January.
  - Oh My Mahjong's International Mahjong Card, played with NMJL rules.
  - Siamese Mah Jongg (Gladys Grad), a separate two-player game with its own January card.
  - Four Friends Mahjong: 4 never-expiring cards with 52 hands, as a teaching tool.
  - The Mahjong Press card in Annual, Starter and **Accessible** versions.
  - I Love Mahj's free custom card.
  - The AMJA card (pictured only).
  — [Sloperama FAQ 7i](https://sloperama.com/mjfaq/cards.html)
- Marvelous borrows concepts from the AMJA card ("cute family names," word-spelling hands). Its rules diverge from the NMJL: quints can use any number in the run, and "A Joker may be picked up to MAH JONGG when used in hands with the letter 'J.'" The AMJA card's hand values are generally higher than the NMJL's. — [Sloperama column 440a](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column440a.html); [Sloperama column 441a](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column441a.html) (from search-result summaries; columns not fetched in full)
- **Wright-Patterson (WP):** officers' wives at what is now Wright-Patterson AFB codified their rules, and "the Wives Club of the Wright-Patterson Officers Club copyrighted their set of rules in 1963 and updates them regularly." WP uses a rules booklet, not an annual card, and is described as more complex than NMJL play. One course listing says hands update about every six years and cites a 2015 edition. Origin dates conflict (1920s vs 1930s). These come from search snippets; the primary booklet was not reviewed. — [Dayton Daily News](https://www.daytondailynews.com/lifestyles/mah-jongg-makes-central-connection/gT6bfhmz4zWEnBP7QbdpgP); [Sloperama column 392m](https://sloperama.com/mahjongg/column392m.html); [Class Central WPMJ lesson](https://www.classcentral.com/classroom/youtube-wpmj-lesson-54556)
- **Multi-card engines already ship:** Mahjong 4 Friends (NMJL + Marvelous); Real Mah Jongg (NMJL + Big Card + Mah Jongg Press); MahJongg4Fun (NMJL + Mahjong Press + Big Card, with per-card completion tracking); The Mahjong App (NMJL + AMS). — [M4F](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1552704332); [Real Mah Jongg](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1249840399); [MahJongg4Fun](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6746219026); [The Mahjong App](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6763795120)
- **Customization precedents:** Eight Bam offers "Open Play to disable NMJL rules and play by your own House Rules." MahJongg4Fun lets users "Make & Validate your hand" and create "private tables with your own rules." Callie's lets users photograph their own card. Daily Mahj offers "your own card or ours." — [Eight Bam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458051621); [MahJongg4Fun](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6746219026); [Callie's](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6781089384); [Daily Mahj](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6798496314)
- **Complexity:** Mahjong Mate says the 2026 NMJL card's "55 lines" create "1059… unique possible winning Mahjong hands." Each printed line is a pattern with suit, number and dragon variables, not a single hand. — [Mahjong Mate](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6478509695)
- **Business precedent for alternative-card publishers in apps:** the American Mah Jongg Association ships its own app, The Winning Hand ($6.99/mo or bundled with membership), although it plays the NMJL card. — [The Winning Hand](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6802114562)

### Inferences
- **Engine design:** a declarative hand-pattern DSL (groups such as pairs, pungs, kongs, quints and singles; suit variables; consecutive-number variables; concealed or exposed flags; point values) plus per-card rule flags (joker eligibility, quint rules, Siamese two-player mode) would cover the NMJL, Marvelous, Big Card, Mahjong Press, AMJA/AMS and WP families. Competitors evidently already have something similar internally.
- **"Users enter their own hands" alone is a weak product.** Typing 50–60 pattern lines a year is a high-friction task for the core 35–70 audience, and reviews show players want the card ready-made. A shareable-import model is also legally muddy: if one user enters the NMJL card and others import it, the app is distributing the card. A better hybrid:
  1. Bundle an original house card, free, as the learning path.
  2. License one or more alternative cards (Marvelous, Mahjong Press, Big Card, AMJA). These publishers are small, already partner with apps, and may welcome revenue share.
  3. Offer a private, local-only "my card" mode with guided pattern entry and a photo reference.
  4. Treat embedding the NMJL card as a counsel-reviewed decision, or seek an NMJL license.
- **WP and Siamese are small niches.** They are cheap to add once the engine is pattern-driven, but not a primary wedge.
