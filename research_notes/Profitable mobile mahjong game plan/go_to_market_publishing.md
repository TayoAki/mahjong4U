## 1. Publisher landscape (2026) and typical deal terms

### Takeaway
Hybrid-casual and puzzle publishing consolidated sharply in 2025–2026. Tripledot (a puzzle-first company) now owns both Lion Studios (via the $800M AppLovin games deal, closed June 2025) and Supersonic (bought from Unity for about $40M, August 2026). Public, current per-publisher revenue-share terms are almost never disclosed. The best hard benchmark is Voyer Law's dataset of 100+ deals: developers average about 58% with an advance and about 68% without one, and keep their IP in 96–100% of deals. Mobile hypercasual/hybrid-casual publishers historically pay out of profit after the publisher funds UA. Vendor sources claim splits as harsh as 70/30–90/10 in the publisher's favor.

### Cited Findings
**Consolidation and publisher status**
- Tripledot completed its acquisition of AppLovin's 10 game studios on June 30, 2025. The deal was worth $800M: $400M cash plus $400M in equity, which gave AppLovin 20% of Tripledot with no board seat. The studios were Athena, Belka, Clipwire, Leyi, **Lion Studios**, Machine Zone, Magic Tavern, PeopleFun, Zenlife and Zeroo Gravity. Tripledot claims nearly $2bn in annual gross revenue, 2,500+ staff, 12 studios and 25m+ DAU. The portfolio includes Wordscapes, Hexa Sort, Project Makeover and Matchington Mansion. — [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/tripledot-completes-landmark-800m-deal-for-10-applovin-studios/); [AppLovin release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250701033978/en/AppLovin-Completes-Sale-of-Mobile-Gaming-Business-to-Tripledot-Studios)
- The price was reported at $900M in early 2025 talks, then $800M at signing. Reports differ on whether the cash included a promissory note. — [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/05/08/tripledot-studios-acquires-applovins-gaming-portfolio-in-800m-deal/); [wnhub](https://wnhub.io/news/marketing/item-47275)
- Tripledot bought **Supersonic** (Unity's hybrid-casual publishing arm, which came from the $4.4B ironSource merger) for about $40M. The deal was announced August 6, 2026. Unity is exiting non-core, lower-margin businesses, including winding down the ironSource Ads network. Supersonic stays a standalone label inside Tripledot. Tripledot's own games include Woodoku, Triple Tile and Nut Sort. It has raised $202M, with a 2022 Series B valuation of $1.4B. CEO Lior Shiff said AI lowers the barrier to making games, which makes discovery harder, and that winning now depends on "UA expertise, capital, and data science/AI capabilities, which small and mid-sized studios increasingly struggle to build alone." — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tripledot-buys-supersonic-for-about-40m-from-unity-exclusive/)
- Lion Studios status: I found no report of a closure. It sits under Tripledot alongside Supersonic. — [gaming.net](https://www.gaming.net/tripledot-buys-supersonic-from-unity-for-40m/); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tripledot-buys-supersonic-for-about-40m-from-unity-exclusive/)
- Scopely signed a definitive agreement in February 2026 to buy a majority stake in Loom Games (Istanbul), maker of the sort-puzzle Pixel Flow. Pixel Flow launched in August 2025 and passed $105M in IAP revenue within about 10 months (AppMagic estimate via Mobilegamer.biz, June 2026). — [GameGrowthAdvisor summary](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)
- Arkadium, a web and casual games company that is strong in mahjong, opened its distribution network to third-party developers in April 2024. In August 2024 it launched third-party publishing ("capital, marketing, user acquisition, promotions, analytics, monetization") with a $1M fund ("APF1"). — [Game Developer press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/arkadium-opens-its-vast-distribution-network-to-developers-worldwide); [BusinessWire title](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20240809092272/en/Arkadium-Unveils-New-Third-Party-Publishing-for-Game-Developers-Alongside-its-%e2%80%98APF1%e2%80%99-%e2%80%93-One-Million-Dollar-Fund/)

**Deal-term benchmarks**
- **Voyer Law, 100+ developer–publisher agreements, 2017–2025, published May 2025 (all platforms, not mobile-specific):**
  - Revenue share: developers average 58.2% (median 50%) with an advance and 67.9% (median 70%) without one. The overall range is 2.5%–90%. The average 2025 deal gave the developer 57.7%, paid in tranches.
  - Advances: average $674,861, median $300,000, range $20K to $6M+. In 93.9% of deals the advance was recouped from the revenue share, and in 48.4% the developer received nothing until recoupment. Milestone payments appeared in 79.7% of deals.
  - IP: retained by the developer in 96.4% of advance deals and 100% of no-advance deals.
  - Term: average 6.84 years with an advance and 4.18 without. 49.2% of deals auto-renew. Sequel clauses appear in 63% of advance deals, and over half of those are exclusive.
  - Source: [GameDev Reports / Voyer Law](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/voyer-law-publishing-agreements-in); [Playio summary](https://blog.playio.co/game-publishing-deal-vs-self-publishing)
- **CrazyLabs (2021, dated):** a developer challenge offered a 55% revenue share to games that hit CPI benchmarks. Its "Publishing For All" program considered games with promising retention or ARPU and **CPI up to $0.80**. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/crazylabs-crazy-winter-developer-challenge); [Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/crazylabs-publishing-for-all-program/)
- **Kwalee (2021, dated):** a challenge offered up to $100,000 in advances plus a publishing deal with "substantial uncapped profit share." — [MCV/search summary](https://mcvuk.com/?p=216659)
- **Homa:** the developer and Homa "agree on a revenue-sharing model." No percentages are published. — [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/09/hyper-casual-game-publisher-homa-games-raises-15-million/)
- **Vendor claims (CAS.AI, April 2026):**
  - "Standard" mobile publisher splits are 70/30 to 90/10 in the publisher's favor, with an example KPI cutoff of D1 ≥ 40%.
  - CAS.AI's own model leaves the developer 100% of baseline revenue and takes 50% of any uplift.
  - Its buyout offers are 12–18× average monthly profit.
  - Source: [CAS.AI blog](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)
- Lion Studios has historically positioned itself as "partner-first" with a revenue-share model (third-party profile). — [theatdb](https://www.theatdb.com/companies/lion-studios)
- GDC 2026 State of the Industry survey (2,300+ developers): 35% self-funded and 20% relied on publishing deals or project financing. — [Playio, citing GDC](https://blog.playio.co/game-publishing-deal-vs-self-publishing)

### Inferences
- Puzzle-capable publishers active in 2026, from this research: Tripledot Group (Tripledot, Supersonic, Lion, PeopleFun for word games), Scopely (Loom), Playtika/SuperPlay (Disney Solitaire), Moon Active (Zen Match), Miniclip/Easybrain (sudoku and logic puzzles), Arkadium (web mahjong), DoubleU Games (Paxie, which has mahjong titles), and CrazyLabs, Homa and Kwalee (historically hyper/hybrid). Voodoo and Azur are cited by vendors as wanting high D1 on prototypes.
- Mobile hypercasual/hybrid-casual publishing deals usually differ from the Voyer premium-game deals. The publisher pays all UA and takes a profit share (revenue minus UA). The practical "developer share" of gross can therefore be far below 50%. Read every term sheet for: whether the share is of revenue or of profit, how UA is netted, recoupment, exclusivity on sequels and clones, and the term or auto-renewal.
- Consolidation means fewer independent puzzle publishers and more "studio inside a portfolio" outcomes. Supersonic's fall from part of a $4.4B merger to about $40M signals that the stand-alone hybrid-casual publishing model has lost value. It does not mean puzzle games have.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) published revenue-share percentages for Supersonic, Voodoo, Homa, SayGames, CrazyLabs, Kwalee, Azur, Rollic, Ruby Games, MAG Interactive, Easybrain, Vita Studio, Wildlife, Playrix, Hungry Studio, MobilityWare or Gameberry. These are negotiated privately and none was found publicly.
- Whether Vita Studio, Learnings, Oakever or Hungry Studio publish third-party games was not confirmed.
- Lion Studios' 2026 operating status (actively signing or folded into Tripledot) is unconfirmed.

## 2. Prototype/marketability tests, soft launch, and prototypes per hit

### Takeaway
Publishers gate on (1) a CPI/creative test with video or playable ads on Meta, AppLovin or TikTok, then (2) D1 retention and playtime, then (3) D7 and LTV in a soft launch. The most-cited thresholds date from the hypercasual era: CPI under $0.30, D1 at least 38% Android, D7 playtime over 2,000s (Supersonic, 2021). Casual-puzzle peer bands are lower (D1 28–32%, D7 7–8% top quartile). A lean self-run soft launch costs about $11K–23K for 4 weeks in one market. A full 12-week program costs about $62K–110K. Hit rates are around 1–2% of launched titles at best, and well below 1% of prototypes at large publishers.

### Cited Findings
**Supersonic's own prototype KPIs (blog dated Aug 2021):**
- Aim for **CPI under $0.30**, **D1 ≥ 38% on Android**, and **D7 cumulative playtime over 2,000 seconds** to move to soft launch.
- No numeric IPM target. Developers submit a store link or video footage.
- Source: [Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/the-3-most-important-kpis-for-testing-your-hyper-casual-prototype)

**Vendor views:**
- Galaxy4Games says 30–35% D1 is enough to open a publisher conversation, 35–40% to scale UA, and 40–45%+ is best-in-class. It also says Voodoo and Azur look for strong prototype D1. — [Galaxy4Games](https://galaxy4games.com/en/knowledgebase/blog/top-companies-offering-hybrid-casual-game-development-services-in-2026)
- CAS.AI cites D1 40%+ as a common publisher KPI cutoff. — [CAS.AI](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)

**Soft-launch budget and kill criteria (GameGrowthAdvisor, Dec 2025, marks which figures are sourced):**
- Media floor: $300–$500 per day per ad set, or $9K–15K per market per month.
- Cohort size: about 1,000 purchased installs per cell (896 installs reads D1 near 30% within ±3 points at 95% confidence).
- Lean run (4 weeks, 1 market, 1 ad set): **$11K–23K total**.
- Full program (12 weeks, 2 markets): **$62K–110K total**. Creative runs $150–$350 per variation.
- Duration: 4–12 weeks, in about 2-week iteration cycles.
- Google Play gate: new personal developer accounts need 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days, which adds 3–5 weeks.
- Specialist UA agencies reportedly decline accounts below about $10K–30K of monthly spend.
- Kill rules:
  - D1 under 25% Android after three builds: stop buying.
  - D7 under 8% or D30 under 3%: stop scaling.
  - CPI at or above LTV with no identified lever: kill.
  - LTV should support CPI at 30–70% of LTV.
  - CPI rising more than 50% when spend triples means the channel is saturated.
- Benchmarks from GameAnalytics (11,600 games): median D1 about 22%; top-quartile D1 25–27% Android and 31–33% iOS; median D7 3.4–3.9%; top-quartile D7 7–8%. The casual/puzzle D1 peer band of 28–32% Android comes from AppsFlyer Q3 2022.
- Source: [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)

**Soft-launch CPI bands for Android casual/puzzle (same source; the author flags the table as an internal reference, not a named dataset):**

| Market | CPI range |
|---|---|
| US | $1.50–3.50 |
| UK, Canada, Australia | $1.00–2.50 |
| France, Germany | $0.60–1.50 |
| Brazil, Mexico | $0.15–0.60 |
| Southeast Asia | $0.20–0.60 |
| India | $0.08–0.30 |

iOS runs about 3–4× these figures (unsourced). — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)

**Hybrid-casual benchmarks:**
- Successful titles show D7 about 20% and D30 about 10% (Lancaric 2025).
- "D7 in the high teens" is where paid UA starts to clear US CPIs.
- Blended ARPDAU is $0.15–0.50 for hybrid versus $0.03–0.08 for hypercasual.
- Prototype-to-launch takes 9–18 months (operator estimate).
- Source: [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)

**Hit rates:**
- AppMagic's H1 2025 casual report: "With a success rate of just 1.4%, only three titles managed to surpass our $100K/month benchmark during H1 2025." This appears to refer to new releases in one casual genre. The page did not render in my fetch, so this comes from a search excerpt. — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-h1-2025/?hl=en)
- CAS.AI vendor claim, unnamed Q4 2025 data:
  - Of released indie games, 1 in 100 reaches $10K/month, 1 in 300 reaches $30K/month, and 1 in 1,000 or fewer reaches $100K+/month.
  - Games that pass initial publisher testing have about a 1 in 10 chance of reaching $30K/month.
  - A successful update yields 4–6% improvement, and only 1 in 6–8 update attempts succeeds.
  - Source: [CAS.AI](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)

### Inferences
- For a mahjong/tile game with an older audience, hypercasual-era thresholds like CPI under $0.30 are not realistic in Tier-1 markets. Mahjong buyers are older US/EU users with higher CPIs and higher LTV. Use casual-puzzle benchmarks instead: D1 at least 35–45% (Vita Mahjong reportedly about 46%, see section 3), D7 at least 15–20%, and LTV of at least 1.5–3× CPI.
- Practical test sequence for a small team:
  1. A creative/CPI test of about $1–3K per concept on Meta or AppLovin in a cheap proxy market such as Canada, Australia or Brazil.
  2. A retention test of about 1,000 installs per build at about $2–5K.
  3. A soft launch at $11–23K for a lean run, iterating in 2-week cycles.
  4. Scale only if the LTV/CPI gate is met.
- Plan for multiple prototypes or "angles" (theme, meta, art style) rather than one game. Even at publishers, very few prototypes reach global launch.

### Gaps
- A widely repeated claim that Voodoo tested about 1,500 prototypes a year against about 4 launches (2023) surfaced in a search excerpt, but I could not verify its source page.
- No 2025–2026 primary disclosure of CPI-test thresholds from Voodoo, Homa, SayGames, Azur, Kwalee or Tripledot.
- No mahjong-specific published prototype KPIs.

## 3. Self-publishing economics and case studies (successes and failures)

### Takeaway
Mahjong solitaire was the breakout casual subgenre of 2025: about 258M downloads, up 189% year over year. Vita Mahjong (Vita Studio, Singapore) made it the 7th most-downloaded game worldwide. But category revenue fell, and Vita has been the single heaviest advertiser on iOS and Android for 7+ consecutive months. This is an ad-funded scale business dominated by deep-pocketed UA buyers. Small self-published mahjong/solitaire games can be profitable at modest scale (Mahjong Titan, an estimated $10K+/month), and ad-supported web solitaire reached about $10K/month. But breakouts (Vita, Zen Match, Tile Busters, Disney Solitaire, Block Blast) were backed by tens of millions in UA, funding or IP.

### Cited Findings
**Market data (AppMagic, Jan 1–Dec 22, 2025):**
- Mahjong solitaire: **258.3M installs, up 189% year over year**. Tile match: **502.9M, up 28%**. **Revenue fell in both categories in 2025** despite the download growth.
- Top titles: Vita Mahjong (Vita Studio) #7 overall with 121.9M; Tile Explore (Learnings) #20 with 92.4M; Tile Club (GameVation) #23 with 86.6M; Block Blast (Hungry Studio) #1 with 356.2M.
- Source: [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-most-downloaded-mobile-games-of-2025/)
- AppMagic's 2026 landscape report lists Vita Mahjong with 126M downloads, up 179% year over year (period unclear in the excerpt). — [AppMagic PDF](https://appmagic.rocks/files/view/upload/Reports/EN_MobileMarkeLandscape2026.pdf)

**Vita Mahjong / Vita Studio:**
- **Ad revenue (Sensor Tower, 19 countries, Jan–May 2026):** Block Blast $127M, **Vita Mahjong $89.8M**, Candy Crush $81.1M. Total mobile-game ad revenue exceeded $12B in 2025. — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/07/06/in-2025-mobile-game-advertising-revenue-exceeded-12-billion-analytics)
- **Ad intensity (SocialPeta):**
  - Vita Mahjong was the most heavily advertised game on both iOS and Android for 7 months running through January 2026. — [Mobilegamer.biz Feb 2026](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-februarys-top-games-vento-gets-4m-vita-mahjong-tops-ad-charts-jest-lands-7m-roblox-stats-more/)
  - In August 2025, Oakever Games' Tile Explorer and Jigsawscapes were the #2 and #3 Android advertisers. — [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-harry-potter-vita-mahjong-delta-force-huuuge-numbers-plus-plinko-and-midcore-market-data/)
- **Company:** Vita Games Studio Pte. Ltd., Singapore. It positions itself as "Designed for Seniors". Its catalogue includes Vita Solitaire, Vita Jigsaw, Vita Color, Vita Word Search and Vita Block. — [Vita Studio](https://www.vitastudio.ai/); [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/dev?id=5970603563954352511&hl=en)

  - Source: [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/1-hour-analysis-vita-mahjong-vita-studio/)

### Inferences
**Break-even math** (illustrative, built from the cited inputs):
- If US iOS puzzle CPI is about $2–4 and the average casual campaign returns about 47% of spend by D30 on iOS, payback requires a long LTV tail. That means at least 2–3× the D30 value over 6–12+ months.
- Example: a $3 CPI needs at least $3 in net LTV. After Apple/Google's 15–30% cut on IAP (ads carry no store fee), you need roughly $3.5–4 in gross per user. At a blended ARPDAU of $0.15, that is about 25 active days per acquired user. Senior-skewed mahjong games with D30 around 10%+ can plausibly reach this. Most new games cannot.

**Minimum realistic self-publish budget:**
- MVP build: about £50–150K (section 4).
- Retention/CPI tests: about $5–10K.
- Lean soft launch: about $11–23K, iterating to a full program at $62–110K.
- Scaling capital: at least $10–30K per month just to be serviceable by UA agencies.
- Bootstrapped all-in to "know if it works": roughly $80–250K. Scaling to a Vita-level business requires millions per month in UA (inference from Vita topping ad charts for 7+ months).

**Mahjong-specific read:**
- Install growth of +189% with falling revenue indicates a crowded, ad-driven category where top players buy volume cheaply via ad-monetized LTV.
- A small team's edge is not to out-spend Vita. It is to find a niche angle in theme, meta or audience (e.g., Tile Busters' obstacles plus 1v1), get ASO/organic and cross-channel distribution (web, Arkadium-style B2B), and keep the costs of a 2–5 person team low.

**What successful small studios did:**
- Vita: obsessive senior UX and minimal interruptions, then aggressive UA.
- Tile Busters: differentiated mechanics and live-ops in a stagnant subgenre.
- Kristanix and Online Solitaire: long-tail evergreen products with low burn.

### Gaps

## 4. Development costs, timelines, and live-ops team size

### Takeaway
Public cost data is mostly from dev-shop marketing pages, so treat it as indicative. A simple puzzle game runs about £10–50K. A polished casual puzzle game with levels and progression runs about £50–150K, built by 3–5 developers over about 6 months. Hybrid-casual prototype-to-global-launch is an estimated 9–18 months. Post-launch costs (marketing, servers, updates, support) can be 30–50% of total budget. Tile Busters reached the top of its subgenre within 8 months of launch, but with a $55M-funded team.

### Cited Findings
- UK studio estimates: a simple puzzle game costs roughly **£10,000–£50,000**, and casual/puzzle titles with multiple levels, progression and polished art cost **£50,000–£150,000**. — [Oceanview Games](https://oceanviewgames.co.uk/blog/posts/mobile-game-development-cost-2026); [Oceanview cost guide](https://oceanviewgames.co.uk/resources/game-development-cost)
- Casual-game baseline: **3–5 developers over about 6 months**. Hypercasual takes about 2 months with 2 people, and a casual 2D game 3–6 months. A playable prototype costs about 10–20% of the full game. Test prototypes start around $8,000. — [Cubix](https://www.cubix.co/blog/mobile-game-development-cost/); [Meliorgames](https://meliorgames.com/game-development/understanding-the-budget-of-mobile-game-development/)
- Marketing, hosting, updates and support often consume **30–50% of total budget** after launch (vendor estimate). — [search summary of dev-cost vendor pages](https://weareaffective.com/learning-centre/how-much-does-it-really-cost-to-develop-a-mobile-game)
- Hybrid-casual prototype to launch: **9–18 months** (operator estimate). — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)
- **Creative production as an ongoing cost (vendor claims):**
  - Large publishers employ 30–40+ motion designers and produce 50–100 video ad variants per game per month. — [CAS.AI](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)
  - Top gaming advertisers run 2,400–2,600 creative variations per quarter (AppsFlyer). — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/game-publishing-deal-vs-self-publishing)
  - Freelance creative costs about $150–350 per variation. — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/)
- Tile Busters went from launch (around October 2022) to top tile-match game in under 8 months, with frequent live-ops events and seasonal content. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2023/12/1/tile-busters-leaving-the-reddest-ocean-to-find-a-blue-spot)

### Inferences
**Mahjong MVP sizing:**
- The core of Shanghai-style mahjong solitaire (tile layouts, a free-tile check, shuffle/hint/undo, a level generator with solvability checks) is technically modest.
- A 2–4 person team (1–2 engineers, 1 artist or outsourced art, 1 designer/PM) could ship a soft-launch MVP in about 3–6 months. Content tooling and level volume (thousands of levels; Tile Club advertises 10,000+) are the main ongoing work.
- Art for tile sets and themes can be outsourced. Budget for continuous ad-creative production, which at scale can rival game-dev cost.

**Live-ops team:**
- Vita shows a low-live-ops model can work for seniors (Gamigion).
- Tile Busters and Royal Match-style games need a dedicated live-ops and content team.
- A plausible small-studio post-launch team is 3–6 people: engineering, level design, art/creative, UA/analytics.

### Gaps
- No published 2024–2026 postmortem with actual cost and headcount for a mahjong or tile game.
- No verified live-ops team sizes from Vita, Learnings, Oakever, Kristanix or MobilityWare.

## 7. Why small casual studios fail, and how successful ones mitigated it

### Takeaway
Failure modes cluster around unit economics: Tier-1 CPIs that exceed LTV, weak D7/D30 retention, too little content or creative throughput, and under-funded testing that stops before signal. Successful puzzle studios mitigated this with an extreme audience focus (Vita's senior UX), differentiated mechanics plus live-ops in a stagnant subgenre (Tile Busters), licensed IP (Disney Solitaire), ad-heavy monetization at huge scale (Block Blast, Vita), or a low-burn long-tail product (Mahjong Titan, Online Solitaire).

### Cited Findings
**Economics:**
- An average casual campaign recovers only 47% (iOS) or 15% (Android) of spend by D30. Average casual D7 ROAS was 5.7% in 2023–24. — [GameDev Reports 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/liftoff-and-singular-casual-games); [GameDev Reports 2024](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/liftoff-and-gamerefinery-casual-games)
- Category saturation: mahjong solitaire and tile-match downloads grew strongly in 2025, but revenue fell in both. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-most-downloaded-mobile-games-of-2025/)
- One dominant buyer (Vita Mahjong) topped iOS and Android ad volume for 7+ straight months. — [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-februarys-top-games-vento-gets-4m-vita-mahjong-tops-ad-charts-jest-lands-7m-roblox-stats-more/)

**Retention:**
- Median D7 is only 3.4–3.9% across 11,600 games (GameAnalytics). Hybrid casual needs about 18–20% D7 for Tier-1 paid UA to work. Kill rules: D1 under 25% after three builds, D7 under 8%, or D30 under 3%. — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/); [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)

**Testing and creative under-investment (vendor claims):**
- Indies spend $10–20K on tests and stop before results are clear.
- They lack a creative pipeline (publishers produce 50–100 ad variants per game per month).
- Update gains are small (4–6% per success, 1 in 6–8 attempts).
- Source: [CAS.AI](https://cas.ai/blog/the-mobile-game-publishing-reality-why-most-indies-fail-and-what-actually-works/)

**Discovery and AI flood:** Tripledot's CEO says AI-lowered barriers mean more titles competing, and winning needs UA expertise, capital and data/AI capabilities "which small and mid-sized studios increasingly struggle to build alone." — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/tripledot-buys-supersonic-for-about-40m-from-unity-exclusive/)

**First-mover decay:** Zen Match led tile-match and then declined as Tile Busters added depth. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2023/10/15/zen-match-how-a-first-mover-falls-behind); [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2023/12/1/tile-busters-leaving-the-reddest-ocean-to-find-a-blue-spot)

**Cancellations:** RedLynx's mobile puzzle game was cancelled about a year after its 2023 soft launch. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2286986/ubisoft-plans-to-close-another-studio-and-lay-off-up-to-122-workers/)

**Mitigations observed:**
- Vita: no ads, offers or lobby in hour one, account-free progress restore, longer and more substantial levels, and a quiet ad-removal IAP. This yielded a reported D1 of about 46%. — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/1-hour-analysis-vita-mahjong-vita-studio/); [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/how-vita-mahjong-stayed-1-in-the-us-app-store-and-what-it-means-for-app-growth-in-2025/)
- Tile Busters: obstacles, an area meta, 1v1 and leagues, many live events, and a UA ramp timed with the feature update. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2023/12/1/tile-busters-leaving-the-reddest-ocean-to-find-a-blue-spot)
- Disney Solitaire: licensed IP, which drove $0.48 IAP per download in week one and about a $300M run rate. — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20250418/amp?f=3); [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/new-disney-title-incoming-from-playtika-as-it-posts-revenue-and-dtc-income-rises/)
- Hybrid monetization (adding IAP to ad-based games) lifts ARPU about 28% versus ad-only (Verve citing AppsFlyer). — [GameGrowthAdvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)

### Inferences
**Decision framing for the team:**
- **Self-publish** if you can fund at least $80–250K through MVP and soft launch, have someone who can run Meta/AppLovin UA and produce creatives weekly, and target a niche the big buyers under-serve (theme, language market, accessibility, offline/no-ads premium, B2B web).
- **Partner with a publisher** (Tripledot/Supersonic/Lion, CrazyLabs, Homa, Kwalee, Arkadium for web) if your prototype shows strong D1 (at least 35–40%) but you lack UA capital and creative throughput. Accept a lower share in exchange for their 10–20% CPI and ARPDAU advantages (vendor claims). Negotiate: IP retained, profit-share definition, no exclusive sequel rights, a short term without auto-renew, audit rights, and kill/termination clauses if the publisher stops UA.
- **Hybrid path:** prove retention cheaply on web/HTML5 (Poki or CrazyGames) and in a low-CPI Android market. Then use the data to negotiate a publisher deal or a B2B syndication deal (Arkadium-type), keeping the mobile rights optional.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024–2026 dataset of puzzle-studio closures with stated causes. Evidence is anecdotal or vendor-sourced.
- No verified 2025–2026 Tier-1 CPI trend specific to mahjong or seniors.
